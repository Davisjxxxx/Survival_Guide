B014 · NORTH WOODS NAVIGATION · P11 · SNOWSTORM / LOST-ROUTE DECISION

WHEN WHITEOUT ERASES THE TRAIL

Visibility failure is a stop signal, not a navigation challenge.

SCENARIO · A familiar track disappears as wind and falling snow remove your reference points.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: On stable ground, reduce wind exposure, keep the group together and make your location obvious to searchers.
IMPROVISED: Use a bright garment for visibility; insulate yourself from snow using safe dry material.
EQUIPPED: Use an offline map, GPS coordinates, whistle or emergency communicator to contact responders.
Four-action sequence
  1. STOP — Stop moving. Gather everyone and name the last verified place.
  2. ASSESS — Check for immediate hazards: exposure, falling branches, unstable banks or traffic.
  3. VERIFY — Retrace only when you can identify the route with reliable evidence and it remains safe.
  4. SHELTER + SIGNAL — If you cannot verify a safe route, remain near a safe location, protect yourself from weather and seek rescue.

Check before continuing

  • Can you name and verify the last known location?
  • Is retracing safer than remaining and sheltering?
  • Is the group protected from wind and wet snow?

Wrong signal → correction

Follow faint tracks → Tracks may be unrelated or erased; use verified landmarks.
Keep walking to stay warm → Uncertain travel risks disorientation and exhaustion.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: No confirmed route, worsening visibility or exposure: stop discretionary travel and seek help. Move only as needed to escape an immediate threat.

B014 · NORTH WOODS NAVIGATION · P12 · CONCEPTUAL MAP / WATER + WETLANDS

THE WATER BARRIER IS NOT A SHORTCUT

A lake, river, beaver pond or bog can interrupt a planned route.

SCENARIO · Your remembered trail appears to lead toward a frozen lake and an unmarked marsh.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: Remain on stable ground; do not test ice strength or mud depth with your body.
IMPROVISED: Mark your own safe stationary location with visible materials. Do not build unverified bridges or rafts.
EQUIPPED: Use current topographic maps and an official trail route; verify them against actual terrain.
Four-action sequence
  1. STOP SHORT — Observe from stable ground, away from unstable banks and shore ice.
  2. READ TERRAIN — Use known map features and visible landmarks to identify water and wetland boundaries.
  3. REJECT RISK — Do not cross uncertain ice, flooded ground or bog to shorten a route.
  4. DECIDE — Choose only a verified safe land detour. Otherwise stay in a safe place and request help.

Check before continuing

  • Is the route an authorized land route, not a water crossing?
  • Are shoreline and bank conditions observed from safe ground?
  • Does the map match the actual landmarks?

Wrong signal → correction

White surface looks walkable → Snow can conceal weak ice and open water.
A blue patch is easy to detour → Wetlands may be larger or less passable than a schematic suggests.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: Unknown ice, deep mud, moving water, unstable shoreline or no verified detour: do not enter.

B014 · NORTH WOODS NAVIGATION · P13 · NO-COMPASS ORIENTATION / LIMITS

NATURE DOES NOT GIVE A SAFE BEARING

Single environmental clues are not dependable navigation instruments.

SCENARIO · You have no compass or charged device and are tempted to use moss or one tree as a north arrow.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: A stationary safe location is better than walking a guessed heading in dense woods.
IMPROVISED: Use a visible note or markers only to describe your current location for rescuers, not to invent a route.
EQUIPPED: Use a reliable map and compass only if you can interpret the map and verify the surrounding terrain.
Four-action sequence
  1. ADMIT UNCERTAINTY — Do not convert a visual hint into a precise direction.
  2. RECONSTRUCT — Recall the last verified trail, signed junction or distinctive landmark.
  3. CROSS-CHECK — Confirm multiple independent features; do not trust one shadow, stream or tree.
  4. STOP IF UNSURE — If you cannot safely identify your route, stop searching, seek shelter and signal.

Check before continuing

  • Can you identify an actual route feature?
  • Are any directional clues independently verified?
  • Would movement increase distance from your last known point?

Wrong signal → correction

Moss faces north → Moss growth depends on local moisture, shade and substrate.
Streams lead to people → A stream can lead into swamps, rapids or more remote terrain.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: No verified direction and no immediate need to relocate: stay in a safe place and seek rescue.

B014 · NORTH WOODS NAVIGATION · P14 · COMPASS / TRUE NORTH / DECLINATION

TWO NORTHS, ONE UNVERIFIED NUMBER

A compass reading and map bearing can differ because of magnetic declination.

SCENARIO · A compass needle aligns to local magnetic north while the map uses geographic north.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: Without a reliable direction, do not guess a compass-equivalent heading.
IMPROVISED: Write down the last known position and reference points. Improvised magnetic needles are not a reliable emergency substitute.
EQUIPPED: Use an appropriate map, an understood compass and a current local NOAA declination value.
Four-action sequence
  1. IDENTIFY NORTH — Read the map legend and the compass reference before taking a bearing.
  2. LOOK UP — Obtain the magnetic declination for that location and date from a verified source.
  3. CHECK METHOD — Use the compass manufacturer’s procedure to convert map and magnetic bearings.
  4. GROUND-TRUTH — Confirm the intended path against reliable terrain features before moving.

Check before continuing

  • Is the declination value location- and date-specific?
  • Are you distinguishing true north and magnetic north?
  • Do observable features agree with your map?

Wrong signal → correction

Copy a bearing from an old guide → Declination changes with time and geography.
Use one compass reading → Local interference or incorrect technique can mislead.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: Unknown declination, unverified map orientation or inconsistent features: do not follow a calculated bearing.

B014 · NORTH WOODS NAVIGATION · P15 · PHONE GPS / MAP / BATTERY

WHEN THE PHONE STOPS HELPING

Offline data, GPS reception, cellular service and battery power are separate dependencies.

SCENARIO · The phone shows low battery, a stale map or no service under heavy forest canopy.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: If the device is unusable, return to the last verified location decision: stop, shelter and signal.
IMPROVISED: Protect the phone against moisture and excessive cold using existing dry layers without hiding it from needed signals.
EQUIPPED: Bring downloaded offline maps, a fully charged power bank, a paper map and an independent emergency communicator.
Four-action sequence
  1. CHECK LOCATION — See whether a trustworthy current position and an already-downloaded map are available.
  2. VERIFY MAP — Compare the display against known junctions and visible features; a dot is not a safe route.
  3. CONTACT HELP — If connected, call 911 or use a supported emergency feature and provide your position.
  4. CONSERVE — Keep the phone protected from cold, minimize nonessential activity and avoid random travel.

Check before continuing

  • Does the map data actually exist offline?
  • Is the position current and consistent with landmarks?
  • Can you communicate without relying on a map app?

Wrong signal → correction

No bars means no location → Position and map availability are separate from cellular data service.
The blue dot proves trail access → A position estimate does not prove a route is passable.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: Missing map, uncertain fix or critically low power: do not navigate on an unverified display.

B014 · NORTH WOODS NAVIGATION · P16 · SIGNALING / FOREST CANOPY

MAKE YOUR POSITION FINDABLE

Searchers cannot always see someone beneath dense forest canopy.

SCENARIO · You are safe enough to remain in place, but the forest conceals you from a road or aircraft.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: Use your voice, bright clothing or deliberate movement that does not put you in danger.
IMPROVISED: Arrange contrasting materials on safe open ground without damaging the environment.
EQUIPPED: Use a whistle, signaling mirror, light or personal locator beacon as appropriate.
Four-action sequence
  1. CALL — Use 911 or an emergency communicator if available; report your location and last known route.
  2. SIGNAL — Use a whistle, visible clothing or a light from stable ground.
  3. STAY TOGETHER — Keep the group at the reported location unless a life threat requires movement.
  4. REPEAT CAREFULLY — Signal when responders are near; conserve battery and energy while awaiting help.

Check before continuing

  • Can a dispatcher describe your actual position?
  • Does a signal risk pushing someone onto water, roads or unstable ground?
  • Can the group remain together and visible?

Wrong signal → correction

Run toward every sound → Unknown direction can disperse the group.
Build a signal fire anywhere → Fire can cause wildfire or injury; avoid without safe legal conditions and skill.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: Do not travel toward distant voices or aircraft unless responders give a safe, verified instruction.

B014 · NORTH WOODS NAVIGATION · P17 · GROUP ACCOUNTABILITY / HUG-A-TREE

IF A CHILD OR PARTNER DISAPPEARS

The lost person and the searching group need different actions.

SCENARIO · At a trail junction, someone discovers a child or hiking partner is missing.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: For a lost child: remain in one safe place near a tree or clear landmark, keep warm and respond to searchers.
IMPROVISED: A child may use bright clothing and a loud voice to signal without wandering.
EQUIPPED: Adults use a phone, whistle, map, group roster and written last-seen details for responders.
Four-action sequence
  1. STOP THE GROUP — Keep everyone else together and account for all members.
  2. VERIFY LAST SEEN — Identify the last confirmed location and time; avoid speculative trail splitting.
  3. CONTACT RESPONDERS — Call 911 promptly with clothing, last-known location, area and medical concerns.
  4. HOLD / FOLLOW — Follow official directions; do not scatter into the woods for an improvised search.

Check before continuing

  • Is every remaining person accounted for?
  • Do searchers have the correct last-known point?
  • Are the child instructions distinct from adult search tasks?

Wrong signal → correction

Everybody fans out → An uncoordinated search can create more missing people.
A child walks toward random voices → A child should stay in a safe place and make themself visible.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: Missing person and uncertain direction: contact authorities early. Avoid off-trail self-directed searches.

B014 · NORTH WOODS NAVIGATION · P18 · OVERNIGHT / EXPOSURE CHOICE

THE LAST LIGHT DECISION

Daylight and weather may run out before an uncertain detour ends.

SCENARIO · The trail is unclear and visibility is decreasing before nightfall.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: Stay out of wind and wet terrain. Avoid unnecessary exertion that increases sweat and heat loss.
IMPROVISED: Use safe natural shelter features and dry materials for wind protection and insulation from the ground.
EQUIPPED: Use a planned emergency shelter, dry layers, headlamp, food/water and an emergency communicator.
Four-action sequence
  1. ASSESS TIME — Estimate remaining usable daylight without assuming a known sunset time.
  2. COMPARE HAZARDS — Check whether travel or staying exposes you to greater immediate danger.
  3. PROTECT — Choose safer stable ground, block wind and preserve dry insulation.
  4. SIGNAL + WAIT — Notify responders when possible; remain findable and monitor everyone for cold stress.

Check before continuing

  • Is remaining in place protected from wind and wet ground?
  • Is anyone wet, confused, unusually sleepy or unable to walk safely?
  • Are responders aware of the likely overnight situation?

Wrong signal → correction

Push on until dark → Navigating uncertain obstacles without light compounds risk.
Sweat heavily to stay warm → Wet clothing increases cold exposure risk.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: When darkness, uncertain route or cold injury changes the risk, stop discretionary travel and seek emergency help.

B014 · NORTH WOODS NAVIGATION · P19 · NAVIGATION FAILURE / CORRECTION ATLAS

SIX WAYS A GOOD-LOOKING ROUTE FAILS

Use the failure atlas to recognize false confidence before moving.

SCENARIO · A single confident assumption can turn a nearby trail into a dangerous detour.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: Do not improvise a heading from moss, wind or stream flow.
IMPROVISED: Use simple notes or signs to describe the last known position, not to invent a track.
EQUIPPED: Use verified map features and a working compass/GPS, cross-checking both.
Four-action sequence
  1. NOTICE — Look for a route assumption rather than a measured fact.
  2. COMPARE — Check terrain, weather, map and navigation tools independently.
  3. CORRECT — Reject unsupported shortcuts and reset to a verified known location.
  4. ABORT — If contradictions remain, shelter and contact help rather than continue.

Check before continuing

  • Do route signs and current terrain agree?
  • Have conditions made an earlier trail or water crossing unsafe?
  • Could one tool, clue or memory be wrong?

Wrong signal → correction

Follow boot tracks → Snow-covered tracks can lead away or vanish.
Cross a white lake → Ice can vary and fail without warning.
Moss means north → Environmental growth is not a bearing.
GPS dot means trail → Coordinates are not passability.
Map omits swamp → Map age/scale and change matter.
Keep walking to search → Random movement increases search area.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: If the navigation evidence is inconsistent, do not guess. Stop in a safe location and request help.

B014 · NORTH WOODS NAVIGATION · P20 · TEAR-OUT / NO GEAR FIRST

LOST IN WINTER: POCKET ACTION CARD

When uncertain, stop discretionary travel and make rescue easier.

SCENARIO · A concise reference for an unfamiliar North Woods route in snow, wind or fading light.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: Stay together. Stay on stable ground. Avoid unknown ice, wetlands and steep banks.
IMPROVISED: Use bright materials for visibility and dry insulation under the body.
EQUIPPED: Use map/compass, offline GPS, whistle, emergency communicator and spare warmth.
Four-action sequence
  1. STOP — Pause. Gather the group and identify immediate threats.
  2. VERIFY — Name the last known place; retrace only if the route is certain and safe.
  3. CONTACT — Call 911 when possible; supply last known point, coordinates and landmarks.
  4. PROTECT + SIGNAL — If you cannot verify a route, remain findable, block wind and await responders.

Check before continuing

  • Immediate hazard addressed?
  • Last-known place recorded?
  • 911 or rescue contact attempted?
  • Group warmth and visibility addressed?

Wrong signal → correction

Guess and keep moving → Stop and reset to the last verified point.
Use an ice shortcut → Keep to known safe land routes.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: No verified route or immediate cold, water or terrain hazard: stop, shelter and seek rescue.
