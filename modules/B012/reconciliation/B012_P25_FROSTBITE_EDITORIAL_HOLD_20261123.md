# B012 / P25 Frostbite: Clinical Editorial Hold

**Status:** Unapproved correction candidate, dated 2026-11-23. **Original P25 HTML and all archives remain unchanged.**

## Exact discrepancy

The existing P25 text states: "If refreezing is ruled out, stable care is available, and warm water is controlled at 37–40°C (99–104°F), follow current first-aid guidance; seek medical evaluation."

Wilderness Medical Society's 2024 field-treatment summary specifies rapid rewarming, **when appropriate**, in controlled water between **37°C and 39°C (98.6°F to 102.2°F)**. Its treatment decision also depends on whether refreezing can be prevented, available resources, and access to definitive care.

Sources:
- WMS, Frostbite Summary 2024: https://wms.org/magazine/1494/Frostbite-Summary-2024/default.aspx
- CDC, Preventing Frostbite: https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/preventing-frostbite.html
- CDC, Hypothermia/Frostbite: https://www.cdc.gov/winter-weather/prevention/

The source reader's upper endpoint is 1°C higher than WMS guidance; the Fahrenheit conversion is also not a precise rendering of the WMS range. Do not interpret this as a validated safe field procedure.

## Proposed replacement for independent medical review

"Suspected frostbite requires medical assessment. Protect the area from pressure, rubbing, and direct heat, and assess for hypothermia. Whether active field rewarming is appropriate depends on safe conditions and the ability to prevent refreezing. When indicated and feasible, expert guidance specifies controlled water at 37–39°C (98.6–102.2°F). If field treatment conditions are uncertain, seek emergency medical guidance rather than improvising a heat source. Do not intentionally keep tissue frozen; avoid a thaw-and-refreeze cycle."
