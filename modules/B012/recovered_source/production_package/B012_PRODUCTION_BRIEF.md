# B012 — Minnesota Winter Survival | Production Brief v1.0

**Status:** Track B preproduction research / visual authoring brief. **NOT** a finished reader chapter, field-tested manual, approved medical guidance, or publication authorization. **Master roadmap:** S00–S15 unchanged; this is Track B012, not an extra master sprint. **Gate A:** BLOCKED. **Local repository:** unchanged.

## Design contract

**Default assumption:** lost or stranded in Minnesota/Upper Midwest winter, minimal equipment, inadequate time, possible wet clothes, low dexterity. Start with safest feasible actions from what exists, improve with salvaged material, and show equipment-assisted upgrades separately. **No primitive replacement is promised where none is reliable.** Photos/diagrams serve the action, not atmosphere. Keep field-danger text visible on the reader page; put source/claim IDs and approval history in repo-side records.

Every reader page must have: **situation → goal → find/choose → 3–6 illustrated actions → verify → failure/correction → stop/escalate → optional upgrade**. Never show untested dimensions, fire clearances, snow-roof strength or presumed temperature gains as numerical facts. Use editable type, real diagnostic photographs, clear cutaways and human-reviewed mechanics.

## Visual-production rules

**Page 27 layout constraint:** show six failure/correction pairs (12 controlled imagery views total) in a legible grid. The other detailed failure cases remain with their subject pages; never compress critical labels below the phone/print readability threshold to fit everything on one page.


1. **Real photo mandatory** for terrain/ice, snow structure conditions, natural bedding materials and cold-injury recognition; include acquisition context, location, date, authentic subject, permission and image-level credit.
2. AI images may **illustrate** controlled steps and cutaways, but cannot establish botanical species, roof stability, injury severity, thermal claims, or field-tested performance.
3. Every depicted sequence must survive a component/mechanics review. Human anatomy, snow depth, load path, wind direction and fire position may not change between steps without explanation.
4. Do not convert a conceptual drawing into an authoritative field build by adding dimensions it cannot substantiate.
5. All important on-page instructions use editable text; AI-rendered words, labels or numbers must not be trusted.
6. Each no-gear method has a **feasibility/energy and abort** gate. A cold survivor may be worse off after hours of unsuccessful construction.
7. Avoid the phrase 'safe ice' and reject lake/river travel as an automatic shortcut; cross-reference B013.
8. No stone fireplace, camp stove, charcoal, flammable fuel or open flame within an enclosed emergency snow/earth/debris shelter.
9. **Land status matters.** Minnesota DNR prohibits digging/trenching a state-forest dispersed campsite and requires permits for cutting/removing many trees/plants. No legal claims copied silently across land types.
10. Design for phone and print, with minimum readable caption sizes and accessible alt text; run page-image QA, version/errata identity and grayscale check before final release.

## Known unsafe or unproven shortcuts to exclude

- Assuming underground/stone-lined shelters are warmer or safer without soil and roof engineering.
- Building an enclosed stone hearth to 'vent' smoke through a small opening; CO remains a fatal risk.
- Treating snow caves as a quick default with no assessment of snow stability or burial/collapse risk.
- Using moss/wet leaves as if dry loft; using live boughs without land-use permission.
- Teaching friction-fire success as guaranteed or allowing prolonged failed attempts while hypothermic.
- Using untreated snowmelt, clarity or improvised filters as proof of microbial safety.
- Giving DIY frostbite/hypothermia treatment instructions that override emergency assessment.
