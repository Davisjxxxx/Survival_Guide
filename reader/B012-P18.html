P18 | B012 WINTER SURVIVAL

Moisture, ventilation and exit checks

Warm-looking shelter can still be dangerously damp

CONCEPT / CUTAWAY

Original schematic. Not a measured or field-tested structure.

Materials and upgrades

No gear: use dry, lofted debris only when safe and available.

Improvised: dry cloth or salvaged waterproof layer with airflow.

Equipped: manufacturer-approved vent and sleep system.

ACTION / DECISION SEQUENCE

  1. Inspect the sleep surface for wetness and compression before resting.
  2. Keep exit usable and ventilation unobstructed, especially in snow or covered shelters.
  3. Separate wet outer layers from the dry insulation zone when feasible.
  4. Recheck wind, condensation, snow accumulation, and occupant condition throughout exposure.
FAILURE: Condensation wets bedding, the entry or ventilation is obstructed, or the frame begins to settle.
CHANGE COURSE: Exit an unsafe enclosure, restore unobstructed air and egress from a safe position, replace wet insulation, and seek more dependable shelter.

LIMIT: A warmer-feeling shelter is not evidence of safe air, dry bedding, or load capacity. Do not use combustion heat in enclosed improvised shelters.
