ZERO EQUIPMENT FIRST · FIELD DECISION

Read Wind, Snow and Hidden Ice

A clear sky does not guarantee good visibility or safe ground.

AT A GLANCE

  • Blowing snow can erase landmarks without fresh snowfall.
  • Avoid frozen water, stream crossings and snow-hidden drainage.
  • Inspect overhead limbs before choosing shelter.

THE FOUR ACTIONS

1

SCAN VISIBILITY

Look downwind for drifting snow, lost landmarks and rapidly changing contrast.

2

SCAN GROUND

Find low spots, creek channels, slush, overflow and shoreline transitions.

3

SCAN OVERHEAD

Avoid damaged trees, ice-laden branches and unsupported dead limbs.

4

FIND A SAFER EDGE

Use terrain and sound forest as wind protection; do not assume a frozen wetland is solid.

RECOGNIZE • CHOOSE • ESCALATE

IF YOU HAVE MORE / LESS

01 · NO EQUIPMENT

Observe wind, footing and visibility from safer ground before committing to movement.

02 · IMPROVISED

Use a loose strip of fabric as a wind cue; mark your safe route only when permitted and reversible.

03 · EQUIPPED

Review NWS alerts and topographic map; use compass/GPS without overriding visible hazards.

WHEN IT GOES WRONG

“It stopped snowing, so travel is safe.”Blowing snow may still cause near-zero visibility; remain protected.
“The lake looks uniformly white.”Do not step out. Snow masks weak and open ice; no ice is completely safe.
STOP / ESCALATE: If visibility collapses, you cannot confirm ground integrity, or you approach unknown ice: stop and choose stable shelter.
