SURVIVAL FIELD ENCYCLOPEDIAB012 / P04 · RESEARCH PROOF
HEAT-LOSS MECHANICS

KEEP DRY. GET OFF THE GROUND.

Snow can drain body heat through contact; wet clothing and wind increase exposure.

Staged material close-up; no thermal performance measurement claimed.
Do this — no extra equipment
  1. Identify cold ground contact, wet clothes, wind, and perspiration.
  2. Find dry, sound insulating material; reject wet or decomposing matter.
  3. Build enough loft beneath the body to resist compression.
  4. Add wind protection; open layers during exertion to limit sweating.
VERIFY
Bed material remains dry and springy when compressed; no skin directly against ice.
FAILURE
Thin bedding collapses and wet plant matter chills the body.
CORRECT
Increase dry loft and replace wet layers; improve overhead weather protection.
STOP: Uncontrolled shivering, confusion, or inability to get warm: stop construction and seek rescue.UPGRADE: Dry mat, spare clothing, insulated sleeping pad.
