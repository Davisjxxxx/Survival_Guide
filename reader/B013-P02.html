B013 · MINNESOTA ICE SAFETY · P02 · IDENTIFY / REJECT

RECOGNIZE ICE TYPES

Appearance is a warning tool, never permission to cross

STAGED SCENE • NOT A RELIABLE ICE-CONDITION IDENTIFICATION PHOTO
New, clear ice is generally stronger than white, refrozen, thawed or deteriorated ice, but strength is not visible with certainty.

HOW THE DECISION OR ACTION WORKS

  1. Identify the surface class — Note clear, white, slush-covered, layered, or deteriorated ice.
  2. Check the season — Freeze-thaw, sun, snow cover, and spring weakening change strength.
  3. Treat unknown layers as unknown — A smooth-looking surface can conceal weak layers.
  4. Reject uncertainty — Do not replace actual thickness measurement with color or sound.

CHECK BEFORE CONTINUING

  • Photo identification is treated as preliminary only.
  • Suspect slush, dark water and thaw conditions trigger a route change.

WRONG SIGNAL → CORRECTION

White surface looks solidSnow ice can be weaker than new clear ice.
No cracks or soundsAbsence of noise does not establish safety.
Ice looks black or clearVisual clarity cannot replace measurement.
STOP CONDITION: If ice strength cannot be verified, stay off. If someone has fallen through, summon emergency responders immediately.
