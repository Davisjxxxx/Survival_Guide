ZERO EQUIPMENT FIRST · FIELD DECISION

Recognize Hypothermia Before Collapse

Shivering is not a reliable measure of how safe the person is.

AT A GLANCE

  • Confusion, slurred speech and stumbling are emergency warnings.
  • A cold person may stop shivering yet be deteriorating.
  • Call 911 immediately for reduced alertness or collapse.

THE FOUR ACTIONS

1

WATCH BEHAVIOR

Notice fumbling, poor decisions, slowed speech, unusual fatigue or drowsiness.

2

CHECK ALERTNESS

Can they answer normally, stay awake and participate? Any impairment is serious.

3

PROTECT CORE HEAT

Shelter from wind; insulate under and around, cover head/neck but keep airway clear.

4

GET HELP

Call 911 for confusion, reduced responsiveness or inability to self-care. Handle gently.

RECOGNIZE • CHOOSE • ESCALATE

IF YOU HAVE MORE / LESS

01 · NO EQUIPMENT

Reduce exposure; position on dry insulation; protect against wind; preserve consciousness and airway.

02 · IMPROVISED

Use dry layers with an outer wind barrier; avoid direct hot objects against skin.

03 · EQUIPPED

Emergency call, insulated wrap, approved active rewarming devices with protection and monitoring.

WHEN IT GOES WRONG

“They stopped shivering, so they improved.”Absence of shivering is not reassurance; judge alertness and other symptoms.
Hot bath, direct heat or rubbing cold limbsAvoid. These can cause harm, burns or dangerous circulatory effects.
STOP / ESCALATE: Any confusion, mumbled speech, failure to remain awake, inability to help remove clothes, or collapse: activate emergency response.
