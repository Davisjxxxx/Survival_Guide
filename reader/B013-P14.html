REACH / THROW: PRACTICE DRY

Learn the movement on stable ground; ice-water rescue itself requires professional training.

HOW THE DECISION OR ACTION WORKS

  1. Choose a safe training area — Use dry, level ground with a cone or stationary target; no frozen pond required.
  2. Practice communication — One participant simulates the call without dialing 911; another directs the team from a safe position.
  3. Practice a controlled throw — Toss a rescue bag or appropriate soft line toward the target while maintaining stable footing.
  4. Inspect and reset — Check line handling, no tangling around fingers or neck, and repeat with a qualified instructor.

CHECK BEFORE CONTINUING

  • All participants and the target remain on dry land.
  • No participant wraps rope around limbs or throat.
  • Every drill rehearses “do not go onto weak ice.”

WRONG SIGNAL → CORRECTION

Tangled ropeRepack the line; inspect before another practice throw.
Thrower steps forwardReset the safe position and rehearse stable balance.
Someone suggests live-water practiceStop; use instructor-led specialized rescue training.
STOP CONDITION: Never turn this dry-land drill into a self-directed ice or open-water rescue exercise.
