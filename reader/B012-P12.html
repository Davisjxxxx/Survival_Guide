ESCALATE CAUTIOUSLY

FROM WINDBREAK TO SHELTER.

Build more only when time, dexterity, dry material, and structural stability permit.

Staged scene; not a field-tested shelter construction photograph.
Do this — no extra equipment
  1. Begin with a safe site, wind reduction, and a dry insulated bed.
  2. Evaluate falling precipitation, temperature trend, remaining energy, and rescue options.
  3. A debris roof may help if sound fallen supports and dry cover can be assembled safely.
  4. Inspect support, overhead load, exit route, and interior dryness repeatedly.
VERIFY
Structure remains stable, exit is clear, floor dry, and no fire is inside.
FAILURE
Heavy roof mass, weak ridgepole, blocked exit, or smoke in a small enclosure.
CORRECT
Reduce roof loads; rebuild only with safe support, or rely on windbreak/insulation and signal.
STOP: Snow caves, tunnels, and excavated roofs need trained stability evaluation; do not use improvised indoor fire.UPGRADE: Certified winter shelter, proper sleeping system, communications.
