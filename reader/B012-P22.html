Snow to water: four separate stages

Snow supplies frozen water, not automatically safe drinking water. Melting solves temperature and handling—not pathogens or toxic chemicals.

NO GEARNo heat-safe vessel or heat source means snow may remain impossible to melt safely.
FOUND MATERIALA sound, suitable metal vessel may enable melting and boiling; do not heat unknown plastics or brittle stones.
EQUIPPEDStove and clean pot support efficient melting; use rated treatment as needed.
IN THE FIELD · FOUR ACTIONS
01Choose a cleaner source

Avoid road splash, fuel, animal waste, tracks and discolored snow; appearance alone is not proof.

02Melt in known safe vessel

If equipped, add a little water before snow so the pot does not scorch; heat only in suitable equipment.

03Treat if quality is uncertain

For microbial risk, boil clear water at a full rolling boil 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft).

04Store treated water

Cool safely; use a clean covered container. Keep untreated collection utensils separate.

HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS
FAILURE → CAUSE → CORRECTION
Pot scorches / snow burnsDry pan heated firstStart with a small amount of liquid water if available; add snow gradually.
Water looks clearMicrobes are invisibleTreat using an effective method; clarity is not validation.
Fuel or chemical contaminationUnsuitable sourceChange source. Boiling is not a chemical-removal process.
STOP / CHANGE COURSE: Do not use snow eating as the hydration plan during serious cold exposure. Do not assume solar warming or a natural filter disinfects melted snow.
