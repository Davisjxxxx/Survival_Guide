SIX FALSE SIGNS OF SAFE ICE

Common observations can be correct but dangerously incomplete.

ORIGINAL IDENTIFICATION/DECISION ATLAS · NOT FIELD TESTED
An observation of appearance, activity or a single test is never a guarantee of safe ice along a proposed route.

FIELD ACTION · NO GEAR FIRST

1
Someone crossed earlierFootprints, ATV tracks and fish houses show activity, not an independent safety assessment.
2
There was a hard freeze last nightSnow cover can slow freezing; inlets, outlets and springs can remain weak.
3
The first drilled hole was thickIce thickness can differ greatly across one lake; the DNR recommends frequent checks.
4
The white surface looks substantialWhite ice is weaker than clear new ice; appearance alone cannot classify every layer reliably.

VISUAL DECISION / METHOD

  1. Someone crossed earlier — Footprints, ATV tracks and fish houses show activity, not an independent safety assessment.
  2. There was a hard freeze last night — Snow cover can slow freezing; inlets, outlets and springs can remain weak.
  3. The first drilled hole was thick — Ice thickness can differ greatly across one lake; the DNR recommends frequent checks.
  4. The white surface looks substantial — White ice is weaker than clear new ice; appearance alone cannot classify every layer reliably.

CHECK BEFORE CONTINUING

  • Do not use the presence of tracks, structures or people as clearance.
  • Do not infer quality from surface color alone.
  • Choose a land-based alternative when conditions are uncertain.

WRONG SIGNAL → CORRECTION

Shore ice looks attachedShallow water, vegetation and warmed structures can weaken edges.
A four-inch number is enoughOnly a conditional minimum for new, clear ice on foot; never an all-purpose guarantee.
A buddy can always rescue meUntrained ice-edge rescues create multiple victims; use professional responders.
STOP: ANY unresolved uncertainty is reason to stay off, not a prompt to experiment.
