B012 · MINNESOTA WINTER SURVIVALFROSTBITE • PREVENT + RECOGNIZE · P08

ZERO EQUIPMENT FIRST · FIELD DECISION

Protect Numb Skin. Avoid Refreezing.

Frostbite recognition cannot depend on skin color alone.

AT A GLANCE

  • Numbness, pain or unusually hard/waxy skin are warning signs.
  • Protect face, ears, fingers and toes from further cooling.
  • Do not rub, massage or apply direct heat to frozen skin.

THE FOUR ACTIONS

1

COVER EARLY

Protect nose, ears, cheeks, fingers and toes before they become numb.

2

NOTICE CHANGE

Pain, numbness, unusual firmness or color changes require attention.

3

MOVE TO SAFETY

Shelter, protect the area from pressure and trauma; also check for hypothermia.

4

SEEK CARE

Get medical assessment. Plan active rewarming only where refreezing can be prevented. Allow spontaneous thawing and protect the area.

RECOGNIZE • CHOOSE • ESCALATE

IF YOU HAVE MORE / LESS

01 · NO EQUIPMENT

Cover vulnerable skin with available clothing, keeping breathing unobstructed.

02 · IMPROVISED

Dry, loose protective fabric and shelter; avoid constrictive wrapping.

03 · EQUIPPED

Dry mittens/boots, insulated face covering and qualified medical care.

WHEN IT GOES WRONG

Frozen fingers rubbed to “restore circulation”Stop rubbing; gently protect from trauma, seek medical care.
Warmed tissue freezes again outdoorsPrevent refreezing; delay active thaw if re-exposure cannot be avoided.
STOP / ESCALATE: Blisters, severe numbness, hard skin, loss of function or coexisting hypothermia require prompt professional assessment.
