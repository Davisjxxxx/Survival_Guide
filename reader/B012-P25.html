Frostbite: Protect Frozen Tissue

Frostbite is a tissue-freezing injury. Signs may include numbness, firm/waxy skin and changes in skin color.

AT A GLANCE

  • Suspect freezing injury if skin is numb, firm, waxy or discolored.
  • Hypothermia takes priority if the person is confused or less responsive.
  • Delay active rewarming if tissue could refreeze. Allow natural thawing and protect the area.
DO NOT RUB • DO NOT BURN • PREVENT RE-FREEZING
1

Get protected

Leave wind and wetness, seek urgent medical advice, and screen for whole-body hypothermia.

2

Reduce further injury

Remove rings or constriction if possible without force. Cover loosely with clean, dry protection.

3

Decide on warming

If re-freezing is possible, protect without deliberately thawing. Seek rescue. Allow spontaneous thawing and protect against refreezing. Never use snow or ice to keep tissue frozen.

4

Rewarm only when appropriate

If refreezing is ruled out, stable care is available, and warm water is controlled at 37–39°C (98.6–102.2°F), follow current first-aid guidance; seek medical evaluation.

DECISION DIAGRAM
  1. Suspected frostbite: protect tissue and assess whole-body cold injury.
  2. If refreezing is possible: keep frozen area protected; do not deliberately thaw.
  3. If no refreezing risk and appropriate controlled water is available: rewarm per first-aid guidance and obtain medical evaluation.
NO EQUIPMENT → IMPROVISED → EQUIPPED

NO EQUIPMENT

Protect the area from cold and friction. Keep hands covered, and avoid walking on frozen feet if possible.

IMPROVISED

Use loose dry fabric. Do not improvise with hot stones, flame or unmeasured near-boiling water.

EQUIPPED

Use a temperature-checked water bath when appropriate; use loose dressings and arrange medical evacuation.

IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
Rubbing or massaging frozen skinStop; handling can cause additional damage.
Hot water, heater, fire or heating pad against numb skinNever use direct heat: burns may occur without warning pain.
Thawing then returning to below-freezing exposureProtect and evacuate; re-freezing can make injury worse.
STOP / ESCALATE: Blisters, blackened skin, extensive numbness, severe pain or uncertainty require medical assessment. Do not puncture blisters.
