Boots, Socks and Mittens: Keep Them Dry

Cold toes and stiff fingers can turn a solvable problem into a rescue.

AT A GLANCE

  • Check for wet fabric, tightness and loss of feeling.
  • Keep hands and feet covered, protected from wind.
  • Do not put numb skin near flames or hot rocks.

THE FOUR ACTIONS

1

CHECK

Are socks damp? Are boot laces tight? Are fingers numb or clumsy?

2

PROTECT

Keep mittens or fabric coverings on; protect exposed nose, ears and cheeks.

3

DRY OR SUBSTITUTE

Use dry spare layers when available; keep wet footwear out of new snow.

4

BEFORE REST

Keep dry socks and insulation protected; prevent footwear from becoming unusably frozen.

RECOGNIZE • CHOOSE • ESCALATE

IF YOU HAVE MORE / LESS

01 · NO EQUIPMENT

Use clothing already worn to shield exposed fingers; stay out of wind and preserve movement.

02 · IMPROVISED

Use genuinely dry salvaged fabric; keep layers loose and do not place wet debris against skin.

03 · EQUIPPED

Insulated mittens, dry spare socks and properly fitted boots; monitor circulation.

WHEN IT GOES WRONG

Wet socks repeatedly freeze in bootsSeek shelter, replace with dry layers if available, and insulate feet off the ground.
Tight wraps or laces reduce warmthEase constriction while retaining secure fit; check feeling and color changes.
STOP / ESCALATE: New numbness, firm or waxy skin, persistent pain, or loss of dexterity may signal cold injury. Do not rub or rapidly heat affected tissue.
