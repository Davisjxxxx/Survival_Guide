B013 · MINNESOTA ICE SAFETY · P11 · VARIABILITY

ONE HOLE DOES NOT CLEAR A ROUTE

Thickness and strength can change over short distances, especially around current.

HOW THE DECISION OR ACTION WORKS

  1. Start with the rule — Minnesota DNR’s 4-inch (10.16 cm) guideline is for new clear ice on foot, never a guarantee.
  2. Identify variability — Consider springs, inlets, narrows, currents, vegetation decay and snow load.
  3. Use correct tools only — Ice checks require suitable measurement equipment and trained judgement, not guesswork.
  4. Abort uncertain travel — Without equipment and a rescue plan, use land instead of trying to prove safe ice.

CHECK BEFORE CONTINUING

  • “New clear ice” limitation remains on all thickness numbers.
  • Old, white, thawed or current-affected ice is not rated by the same simple rule.
  • Retreat or stay ashore is a real option.

WRONG SIGNAL → CORRECTION

4 inches at one pointNearby ice may be much thinner.
Four inches of white iceDNR clear-ice guidance does not transfer directly.
Someone else is fishingTheir location does not verify your route.
STOP CONDITION: If any segment cannot be evaluated, do not cross.
