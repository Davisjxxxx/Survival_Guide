# North Woods navigation foundations · Reconstruction r01

New Z11/B014 draft, 2026-11-24. Historical P01–P10 titles, wording, illustrations and order remain unknown. The ten slots below are proposed identifiers. The retained P11–P20 supplement keeps its numbering and source.

## Reconstruction brief

Write for a walking party in Minnesota's North Woods. Start with preparation and terrain recognition. Use paper-map and compass exercises before introducing cold-weather electronics and the supplemental failure scenarios. Each finished entry needs a context, required knowledge, a way to verify the next decision, a stop condition and a source locator. Practice exercises require safe dry land, known boundaries and a navigation reviewer. Use an original schematic or a rights-checked map excerpt. Real map imagery needs date, sheet, scale, credit and a caption describing the task.

## R01 · Leave a trip plan

**Where it applies.** A planned day outing on a Minnesota state forest trail. Record your trailhead, intended route, party size, planned return time and a way to recognize your vehicle or group. Give the plan to a trusted person before you leave. Agree on when an overdue return should trigger action and how to pass the plan to emergency responders.

**Check.** Ask your contact to repeat the trailhead and overdue time. Keep a copy with an offline map. Discuss a turnaround point before starting. If the route changes, communicate the update when possible. If essential details are uncertain before departure, revise the plan at the trailhead.

**Visual brief.** An editable sample trip-plan panel with fictional locations, route and time fields. A reviewer checks that the plan works with intermittent service and a missed call.

**Source locator.** [Minnesota DNR, Safety in state forests](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/state-forests/safety.html), trip preparation and route planning. The read-back and example panel are editorial proposals.

## R03 · Identify the map's north reference before taking a bearing

Look up the sheet date, scale and legend. Identify the north reference used by the map and whether any declination information printed on the sheet is dated. Magnetic declination is the angle between magnetic and true north and varies with time and location. Confirm the setting for the location and date during trip preparation. In a practice area, compare your map reading against a visible feature you can name on the map. Pause if the feature, map reference or compass setup remains uncertain.

**Visual brief.** Use a clearly labelled conceptual sheet that distinguishes grid, true and magnetic north. Provide an accompanying plain-text description. A reviewer verifies the diagram and instrument convention before any numeric example is drafted.

**Source locator.** [NOAA NCEI, Magnetic Declination](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/magnetic-declination), definition and bearing conventions.

**Acceptance.** Navigation and SAR specialists review action wording, offline exercises, stop conditions and role assignments. Compare each proposed entry against an original if it becomes available. The remaining eight slots are briefs.
