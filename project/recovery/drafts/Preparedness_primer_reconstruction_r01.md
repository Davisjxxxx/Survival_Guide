## R01 · Write one contact and evacuation sheet

Keep a printed sheet with the household meeting point, an out-of-area contact, a backup contact method and current local alert channels. Record specific needs for people and pets and identify who can help. List a primary and alternate destination with travel constraints. Review both routes using authoritative local instructions and decide how changes will be communicated. In a flood warning, follow official evacuation instructions and stay out of flooded roads.

**Practice check.** Give each household member a copy. Ask them to locate the meeting point and contact information and explain what they will do when the usual route is closed. If a destination or route is uncertain, update the sheet before the next drill.

**Evidence and review.** The contact and evacuation ideas come from the official FEMA flood information sheet. The printed worksheet and practice check are editorial drafts. A local emergency management reviewer should validate the scenario and alert routing. [FEMA flood hazard information sheet](https://www.ready.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/fema_flood-hazard-info-sheet.pdf)

## R02 · Start an outage needs inventory

List the essential services the household relies on and the time-sensitive needs each one supports. Record what can be handled at home, who can provide support, and at what point you must contact the provider or relocate. Start with medications, mobility, communications, drinking water and household supplies. Make responsibility and the next check time explicit. Confirm contacts using their published numbers and keep the list available offline.

**Editorial boundary.** Utility work, medical equipment, heating and food decisions require device- or jurisdiction-specific sourced entries and qualified review. Create separate evidence cards rather than attempting to generalize from this worksheet.

**Evidence and review.** FEMA calls out emergency supplies, communication, medications and the needs of household members. The time-trigger worksheet extends those ideas and requires scenario review. [FEMA flood hazard information sheet](https://www.ready.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/fema_flood-hazard-info-sheet.pdf)
