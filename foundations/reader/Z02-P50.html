Z02-P50 · Shelter and housing · New manuscript

Keep fuel stoves, lanterns and heaters completely outside tents and vestibules

Enforce a zero-combustion enclosure rule to prevent fatal carbon monoxide poisoning and catastrophic shelter fires.

When it applies · Tents, tarps with enclosed sides, campers, trailers, cabins without vented heating, snow shelters and vehicles.

Equipment · Outdoor cooking area clear of fabric, battery/LED lighting, dry thermal insulation and CO alarm where applicable.

Actions

  1. Never ignite, operate or smolder fuel-burning stoves, catalytic heaters, fuel lanterns or charcoal grills inside tents, tent vestibules, snow caves, campers or vehicles.[C05, C16]
  2. Recognize that cracking a tent door, roof vent or garage door does NOT prevent lethal carbon monoxide accumulation—and high altitude worsens incomplete combustion.[C05, C16]
  3. Separate heat and light completely from combustion inside sleeping spaces: use battery LED headlamps for light and dry layers plus ground pads for warmth.[C05, E02, E05]

Failure recognition · Boiling snow on a canister stove inside a zipped tent vestibule during a storm, causing invisible odorless CO buildup or a flash fabric fire.[C05, C16, C15]

Verify · Confirm zero fuel appliances, charcoal or open flames operate inside any shelter or vestibule and that all cooking runs outdoors downwind.[C05, C16, C15, ED]

Stop or change course · Headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness or confusion in or near any shelter with fuel use nearby requires immediate exit to fresh air and calling 911.[C07, C16]

Section: Wildland Fire Safety > Campfires and Stoves · Conditions: Outdoor cooking with portable liquid or canister stoves
Support: Operate stoves on level cleared ground away from tents and dry fuels, cool completely before refueling, and move away from any spilled fuel before lighting.
Limitations: Fuel canisters behave differently in cold weather and high elevation; follow manufacturer limits. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with CPSC-C and USFS-FIRE.
