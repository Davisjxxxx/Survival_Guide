Shelter safely inside a stranded winter vehicle and guard against exhaust CO

Survive being snowbound in a vehicle without wandering to death in whiteouts or dying silently from snow-blocked tailpipe CO.

When it applies · Vehicles stuck in ditches, highway closures, or rural snowdrifts during blizzards and extreme cold.

Equipment · Vehicle winter kit, shovel, bright signal cloth, phone/911, and strict tailpipe inspection routine.

Actions

  1. STAY WITH YOUR VEHICLE if stranded in a winter storm unless a clearly visible, open, heated building is literally yards away—wandering on foot into blowing snow disorients travelers within seconds and causes fatal hypothermia.[E14, D10]
  2. When safely stranded outside, NWS describes running the engine for about ten minutes each hour if the tailpipe is clear and a window is open slightly. Check conditions first. Do not run with blocked exhaust or suspected CO.[D19, C07]
  3. Before running the engine for brief 10-minute-per-hour warming cycles, step out ONLY if safe to PHYSICALLY CLEAR SNOW FROM THE ENTIRE TAILPIPE AND UNDERBODY, crack a downwind window slightly for fresh air, and NEVER let everyone sleep while the engine idles (snowdrifts plug tailpipes silently and fill cabins with lethal CO!).[E14, C05, C07]

Failure recognition · Leaving a stranded car's engine running continuously all night while occupants sleep as drifting snow seals the exhaust pipe beneath the bumper.[E14, C05]

Verify · Confirm 911 notified, tailpipe confirmed 100% snow-free before any brief engine cycle, downwind window cracked, and bright marker visible.[E14]

Stop or change course · If the exhaust cannot be kept clear, do not run the engine. If CO is suspected, turn it off only if this does not delay reaching fresh air and contacting 911. Tell dispatch about any unsafe conditions outside the vehicle.[D19, C07]

C07 · CPSC · Carbon Monoxide Fact Sheet (current web edition)
Section: CO poisoning symptoms; what to do if suspected · Conditions: Suspected carbon monoxide exposure
Support: Escape to fresh air immediately and contact emergency services. Symptoms can include headache, dizziness, nausea and confusion.
Limitations: CO is colorless and odorless; symptoms mimic viral illness or altitude fatigue and can incapacitate sleepers without waking. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with CDC-CO.

D10 · FEMA / Ready.gov · Winter Weather (current web edition)
Section: Blizzards, Ice Storms, and Multi-Day Freezing Blackouts · Conditions: Blizzard Warnings, Ice Storm Warnings, and extreme wind-chill emergencies
Support: Complete supply, fuel, and medication staging before storm onset, stay off snow/ice-closed roads, prepare safe non-combustion warmth consolidation indoors, keep vents/exits clear of snowdrifts, and evacuate to community warming shelters early if indoor heat fails.
Limitations: Ice storms down overhead power lines and trees simultaneously, delaying utility restoration for days across wide areas. · Conflicting evidence: None; connects Z15 winter disaster response with Z12/Z13.
Editorial note: Grounds winter-storm travel halts and warming-center transitions. · Specialist reviewer: Cold-weather emergency management
