Z10-P39 · Wilderness medicine · New manuscript

Shield blunt or penetrating eye trauma without applying pressure

Prevent catastrophic vision loss from eyeball pressure or removal of embedded objects after sharp or blunt eye strikes.

When it applies · Branch pokes, flying metal/wood chips, fishing hooks, or hard blunt blows to the eye socket with pain, blood, or vision change.

Equipment · Rigid makeshift eye shield (clean paper cup bottom or commercial eye shield), tape, and emergency communications.

Actions

  1. Never press on an injured eyeball, never flush a suspected ruptured/penetrated globe, and NEVER pull out any embedded splinter, thorn, glass, or fishhook from the eye.[M18]
  2. Tape a rigid dome shield (such as the clean bottom of a paper cup or commercial shield) gently over the bony brow and cheekbone so NOTHING touches the eyelids or protruding object, and ask the patient to keep both eyes calm and still to reduce synchronized eye movement.[M18]
  3. Keep the patient sitting slightly upright if comfortable, keep NPO for potential surgery, and evacuate immediately to emergency ophthalmology care.[M18, M05, T02]

Failure recognition · Packing tight gauze pressure directly over a branch-punctured eye or trying to pull a barbed fishhook out in the field.[M18]

Verify · Confirm rigid shield rests only on surrounding bone with zero eyelid contact, embedded objects remain untouched, and urgent EMS/transport is active.[M18, T02]

Stop or change course · Any penetrating object, visible blood inside the colored iris, double vision, or vision loss is an immediate sight-saving emergency.[M18, T02]

Evidence and editorial notes
M05 · NIH / MedlinePlus · Shock (current web edition)
Section: Recognizing Circulatory Shock and Positioning · Conditions: Injured or ill patient with pale cool clammy skin, rapid weak pulse, confusion, or dizziness
Support: Treat suspected shock as a life-threatening emergency: call 911, control external bleeding, keep the person warm and insulated from cold ground, and do not give food or drink.
Limitations: Field responders cannot diagnose internal hemorrhage or administer IV fluids. · Conflicting evidence: None; reinforces keeping suspected shock patients NPO and warm.

M18 · NIH / MedlinePlus · Eye Injuries (current web edition)
Section: Eye Injuries, Flushing, and Penetrating Trauma Limits · Conditions: Dust/chemical splash vs blunt or penetrating eye trauma
Support: Flush chemical splashes or loose surface grit gently with clean potable water or sterile saline; never rub the eye, never remove an embedded object, shield without pressure, and seek urgent eye care.
Limitations: Forcing open swollen eyelids or pulling embedded debris causes irreversible vision loss. · Conflicting evidence: None; separates surface irrigation from penetrating-eye shield protection.
Editorial note: Core eye-trauma decision boundary in Z10. · Specialist reviewer: Ophthalmology and emergency medicine
Primary source · checked 2026-11-24
T02 · American Red Cross · First Aid Steps (current web edition)
Section: First Aid Steps > Call 9-1-1 · Conditions: Lay emergency response to unresponsiveness, abnormal breathing or severe bleeding
Support: Activate EMS and get needed equipment. Follow dispatcher directions and act within training.
Limitations: Remote locations may delay EMS arrival, requiring sustained stabilization after activation. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with NPS-E.
Editorial note: Mandates early emergency activation and dispatcher adherence. · Specialist reviewer: EMS and wilderness medicine
