Z04-P16 · Water · New manuscript

Assess moving streams versus stagnant ponds and pools

Compare physical, biological and sediment trade-offs between flowing streams and standing water bodies.

When it applies · Selecting a raw backcountry water collection point after chemical and algal-bloom screening.

Equipment · Raw-water scoop or vessel, pre-filter cloth and validated downstream microbial treatment.

Actions

  1. Prefer steadily moving, oxygenated channels away from shoreline debris over stagnant warm pools that concentrate organics, silt and algae.[W24, W16, ED]
  2. Recognize that moving streams still carry Giardia, Cryptosporidium, bacteria and storm-driven turbidity from upstream wildlife and soil erosion.[W09, W19, W20, W24]
  3. If only standing pond or lake water is available, inspect carefully for algal blooms, draw gently without stirring bottom mud and pre-clarify before treatment.[W15, W08, W24]

Failure recognition · Assuming fast-flowing cold streams are sterile or dipping straight into warm stagnant margins that clog filters and harbor cyanotoxins.[W09, W15, W24]

Verify · Confirm no chemical or algal hazards exist and identify the exact clarification and microbial treatment steps matched to the chosen source.[W01, W15, W08, ED]

Stop or change course · Reject stagnant water with algal scum, dead animals, oily sheen or foul chemical odor; relocate to a safer source or seek assistance.[W01, W15, W17]

W15 · CDC · Harmful Algal Bloom (HAB)-Associated Illness (current web edition)
Section: Harmful Algal Blooms > Drinking Water and Exposure · Conditions: Surface water with discolored scum, mats, foul odor, or active HAB advisory
Support: Avoid contact and do not drink water from suspected harmful algal blooms; boiling does not destroy cyanotoxins and can concentrate them as water evaporates.
Limitations: Cyanotoxins can persist in visually clear water after a bloom dissipates. · Conflicting evidence: Contradicts naive boil-everything advice; CDC/EPA HAB warnings explicitly prohibit boiling HAB water.
Editorial note: Establishes HABs as a chemical-toxin rejection trigger, not a microbial boil target. · Specialist reviewer: Environmental health and drinking-water safety

W19 · CDC · Preventing Giardia Infection (current web edition)
Section: Preventing Giardia Infection > Backcountry Water · Conditions: Untreated surface water in wilderness and rural watersheds
Support: Giardia cysts survive in cold freshwater and resist brief or low-dose chlorine exposure; boil or use a filter specifically rated for cyst removal (e.g., 1 micron absolute or NSF 53/55 cyst reduction).
Limitations: Pore size alone does not protect if cartridge seals leak or freeze-crack. · Conflicting evidence: None; underscores why chemical-only treatment has parasite limits.
Editorial note: Clarifies why filter micron/NSF ratings matter for protozoa. · Specialist reviewer: Drinking-water microbiology and public health

W20 · CDC · Preventing Cryptosporidium Infection (current web edition)
Section: Preventing Cryptosporidium Infection > Water Treatment · Conditions: Surface water impacted by wildlife, livestock, or runoff
Support: Cryptosporidium oocysts are highly chlorine-tolerant; boiling or certified cyst-rated filtration (absolute <= 1 micron or NSF 53/55) is required rather than standard chlorine alone.
Limitations: Standard bleach or chlorine tablets at routine doses do not reliably inactivate Cryptosporidium. · Conflicting evidence: Clarifies gap left by single-barrier chlorine disinfection.
Editorial note: Core evidence for filter-plus-disinfect multi-barrier design in Z04. · Specialist reviewer: Drinking-water microbiology and public health

W24 · USGS · Turbidity and Water (current web edition)
Section: Turbidity and Water · Conditions: Cloudy, silt-laden, or storm-runoff streams and lakes
Support: Turbidity consists of suspended clay, silt, organic matter, and plankton that scatter light, shield pathogens from UV and disinfectants, and rapidly clog fine filter elements.
Limitations: Low turbidity does not establish biological or chemical safety. · Conflicting evidence: None; supports physical mechanism of pre-clarification.
Editorial note: Explains physical failure of filters and UV in turbid storm runoff. · Specialist reviewer: Hydrology and drinking-water treatment
