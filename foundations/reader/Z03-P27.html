Z03-P27 · Fire and ignition · Adapted historical material

Trace friction-ember dust formation and notch geometry in wood drills

Explain the mechanical role of wood dust accumulation, hearth notch shape and downward pressure in friction fire sets.

When it applies · Conceptual mechanism study and supervised training; preserves historical B008/B010 mechanism plates separately without claiming field-validated emergency success.

Equipment · Identified training bow-drill or hand-drill components inspected cold (hearth board, spindle, notch, catch leaf, bearing block).

Actions

  1. Trace how downward force plus reciprocating rotation grinds fine dry wood dust at the spindle-hearth socket and heats that dust through mechanical friction.[C12]
  2. Identify the V-shaped hearth notch as the dust collection channel: it lets heated dark-brown wood powder spill into one compact pile where insulated heat can self-sustain as a glowing ember.[C12, ED]
  3. Recognize that visible white smoke during drilling only signals warming wood—not yet a viable ember; stopping too early or disturbing the fragile dust pile collapses ignition.[C12, ED]

Failure recognition · Expecting an un-notched hearth hole to produce an ember, or spending hours grinding wet unknown woods while hypothermia sets in.[C12, C08, E06]

Verify · Point to the bearing block, rotating spindle, hearth socket, V-notch dust pile and catch surface on a cold unlit assembly.[C12, ED]

Stop or change course · Treat friction drilling as a supervised training skill, not a primary cold-weather survival guarantee; abort immediately if effort causes sweating, exhaustion or worsening exposure.[C12, C08, E10]

Section: Background and Procedure > Bow Drill · Conditions: Interpretive demonstration using known materials
Support: Reciprocating motion turns a spindle against a hearth. Friction heats wood dust until it can sustain an ember.
Limitations: Moisture, resinous wood, poor notch geometry, or physical fatigue can prevent ember formation. · Conflicting evidence: Does not guarantee ignition in emergency field conditions; conceptual/supervised training only.
