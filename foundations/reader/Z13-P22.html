Z13-P22 · Home survival and utilities · New manuscript

Maintain wood stoves and chimneys safely to prevent creosote flue fires

Operate properly installed residential wood heat cleanly while preventing explosive chimney fires and indoor smoke backdrafts.

When it applies · Homes and cabins with code-installed, inspected wood-burning stoves or masonry fireplaces.

Equipment · Annual certified chimney-sweep inspection record, dry seasoned hardwood, stove-pipe thermometer, hearth pad clearance, and smoke/CO alarms.

Actions

  1. Have every wood stove, flue pipe, and chimney inspected and cleaned annually by a qualified chimney professional BEFORE winter heating season to remove flammable creosote buildup and animal nests.[U18, U03]
  2. Burn ONLY dry, seasoned split firewood in brisk hot fires—never burn green wet wood, cardboard, glossy paper, painted/pressure-treated lumber, or household trash (which coats flues with creosote and releases toxic fumes).[U18]
  3. Use the appliance listing, manufacturer instructions and local requirements for floor protection, clearances and operating configuration. Use a qualified professional for installation and inspection.[U18, U03]

Failure recognition · Burning damp unseasoned pine and wrapping paper in an uncleaned chimney until thick black creosote ignites into a roaring flue fire.[U18]

Verify · Confirm the professional inspection, dry seasoned wood, appliance-specific clearances and working smoke and CO alarms.[U18, U12]

Stop or change course · Loud roaring/rumbling sound in the flue, glowing red stovepipe, dense indoor smoke, or exterior chimney sparks require evacuating everyone immediately and calling 911.[U18, U04]

Evidence and editorial notes
U03 · NFPA · Heating Fire Safety (current web edition)
Section: Heating Equipment Safety > Space Heaters, Fireplaces, and Clearances · Conditions: Residential winter heating and supplemental backup heating
Support: Maintain at least a 3-foot clearance between anything flammable and any heating source (space heaters, fireplaces, wood stoves), inspect chimneys annually, use only the fuel specified by the heater manufacturer, and never use kitchen ovens to heat homes.
Limitations: Unvented fuel-burning heaters also create severe carbon monoxide and indoor air hazards; local building codes govern installations. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with CPSC-C and READY-HOME-F.

U04 · FEMA / Ready.gov · Home Fires (current web edition)
Section: Home Fires > Smoke Alarms, Fire Extinguishers, and Escape Plans · Conditions: Residential fire prevention during outages and supplemental heating
Support: Test smoke alarms monthly on every level and outside sleeping areas, keep two exits clear from every room, store fuels outside living spaces in approved containers, and evacuate immediately if a fire exceeds incipient containment.
Limitations: Candles and improvised indoor flames dramatically elevate outage house-fire deaths. · Conflicting evidence: None; integrates with CPSC CO-alarm rules (C09).

U12 · CPSC · CO Alarms (current web edition)
Section: Home Alarms > Smoke, Carbon Monoxide, and Battery Backup Readiness · Conditions: Occupied residences with fuel-burning furnaces, water heaters, attached garages, or outage generators
Support: Maintain interconnected or battery-backed CO and smoke alarms on every floor and outside sleeping rooms, replace expired sensors on schedule, and keep exterior furnace/dryer/exhaust vents clear of drifting snow.
Limitations: Blocked high-efficiency PVC furnace intake/exhaust pipes in heavy snow can force CO back indoors or lock out heating systems. · Conflicting evidence: None; integrates with C09 and Z13-R03.

U18 · NFPA · Heating Fire Safety (current web edition)
Section: Wood Stoves, Chimneys, Ash Disposal, and Fuel Storage Safety · Conditions: Residences heated by properly installed wood stoves or fireplaces
Support: Burn seasoned dry wood only, clean creosote-prone chimneys regularly through qualified sweeps, empty cooled ashes strictly into lidded metal containers set outdoors well away from decks and structures, and never burn treated lumber or trash indoors.
Limitations: Embers stay hot enough to ignite cardboard or wood decks days after a fire appears dead. · Conflicting evidence: None; critical winter wood-heat fire prevention boundary.
Editorial note: Covers solid-fuel residential heating maintenance and ash-fire failure modes. · Specialist reviewer: Fire safety and certified chimney/HVAC trades
