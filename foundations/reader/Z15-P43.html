Z15-P43 · Disaster scenarios · New manuscript

Manage multi-day electrical outages safely without indoor combustion or fires

Sustain safe lighting, food triage, temperature control, and battery conservation across 3-to-14-day regional blackouts.

When it applies · Multi-day residential power loss across winter or summer disaster scenarios.

Equipment · LED lanterns, UL battery power banks, outdoor-only ≥20 ft generator (if owned), CO/smoke alarms, and appliance thermometers.

Actions

  1. Establish a calm daily blackout rhythm right away: switch to low-mode LED lighting (zero candles), log power-off times and guard refrigerator/freezer doors, unplug surge-sensitive electronics/heaters, and conserve smartphone power strictly for brief SMS check-ins and 911.[U25, U01, U04, F04, U16]
  2. Enforce ZERO indoor combustion compromises across every day of the outage—keep portable generators ≥20 feet outdoors with exhaust away from structures, cook strictly outdoors on grills/camp stoves (or eat no-cook pantry meals), and never heat homes with gas ovens or charcoal.[U25, C06, C16, U02]
  3. Track household health, indoor room temperature, water supply, and fuel/battery burn rate daily so you relocate cleanly to powered shelter or community hubs BEFORE reserves hit zero.[U17, D18]

Failure recognition · Staying disciplined on day one of a blackout, then pulling a generator into the garage on day four out of fatigue and rain frustration.[C06, C16]

Verify · Confirm zero indoor combustion, active battery CO/smoke alarms, logged fridge/freezer temps, and daily reserve tracking.[C06, U12, F04]

Stop or change course · Any indoor CO alarm, unsafe cold/heat indoors, or exhausted medical power ends home sheltering immediately in favor of relocation or 911.[C07, U17]

Section: CO poisoning symptoms; what to do if suspected · Conditions: Suspected carbon monoxide exposure

Limitations: CO is colorless and odorless; symptoms mimic viral illness or altitude fatigue and can incapacitate sleepers without waking. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with CDC-CO.

C16 · CDC · Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Basics (current web edition)
Section: Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Basics > Sources and Prevention · Conditions: Any enclosed or semi-enclosed structure, garage, porch, tent, or vehicle
Support: Never burn charcoal, operate camp stoves, or run fuel-burning heaters inside homes, garages, tents, or vehicles even with doors or windows partly open.
Limitations: Vent cracks and open garage doors do not prevent lethal CO accumulation. · Conflicting evidence: Refutes dangerous myth that cracking a tent vent or garage door makes indoor combustion safe.

Section: Home Alarms > Smoke, Carbon Monoxide, and Battery Backup Readiness · Conditions: Occupied residences with fuel-burning furnaces, water heaters, attached garages, or outage generators
Support: Maintain interconnected or battery-backed CO and smoke alarms on every floor and outside sleeping rooms, replace expired sensors on schedule, and keep exterior furnace/dryer/exhaust vents clear of drifting snow.
