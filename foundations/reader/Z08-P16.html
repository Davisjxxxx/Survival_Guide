Cleavage Planes vs Conchoidal and Hackly Fracture

Use breakage geometry to separate minerals that split along atomic crystal planes from hard siliceous rocks that fracture conchoidally.

When it applies · Field mineral diagnostics on naturally broken edges.

Equipment · 10x hand lens, bright daylight, notebook.

Actions

  1. Rotate a clean natural face in sunlight to catch flat, parallel, mirror-like reflections indicating true atomic cleavage planes.[R04]
  2. Count cleavage directions and rough angles—such as one basal sheet in mica, two near-90-degree planes in feldspar, or rhombs in calcite—before consulting a key.[R04]
  3. Confirm curved shell-like ripples without repeating flat steps as conchoidal fracture typical of quartz, chert, and natural glass.[R04, R05]

Failure recognition · Mistaking a single flat crystal face or flat fracture for true repeating cleavage misidentifies quartz as feldspar or calcite.[R04]

Verify · Lighting tilt check confirms whether parallel stepped reflections repeat across the mineral body.[R04]

Stop or change course · Stop breaking specimens to create fresh cleavage faces on protected lands or without eye and dust controls.[R06, R08]

Evidence and editorial notes
R04 · USGS · Collecting Rocks: Rock Classes and Mineral Property Basics (USGS GIP)
Section: Hardness, luster, streak, and fracture vs cleavage · Conditions: Non-destructive mineral observation with simple field cues
Support: Scratch hardness, unglazed streak, luster, and fracture-versus-cleavage patterns narrow candidate mineral groups.
Limitations: Field scratch checks cannot establish chemical purity, trace heavy metals, or absence of hazardous fibers. · Conflicting evidence: Color alone is unreliable across quartz, calcite, and feldspar varieties.
Editorial note: Core Z08 mineral screening basis. · Specialist reviewer: Mineralogist

R05 · Minnesota DNR · Rocks and Minerals of Minnesota Educational Guide (Current web edition)
Section: Chert, jasper, agate, and quartz occurrences · Conditions: Upper Midwest siliceous cobbles and sedimentary nodules
Support: Cryptocrystalline quartz varieties (chert, flint, jasper, agate) are hard silica rocks showing conchoidal fracture rather than planar cleavage.
Limitations: Visual similarity to fine-grained quartzite, basalt, or glass does not confirm thermal stability or structural suitability. · Conflicting evidence: None noted.
Editorial note: Anchors Z08 siliceous stone identification. · Specialist reviewer: Geologist and materials specialist
Primary source · checked 2026-11-25
R06 · NIOSH / CDC · Crystalline Silica Respiratory Dust Hazards (2024)
Section: Crystalline silica dust respiratory hazard · Conditions: Any grinding, hammering, knapping, or crushing of quartz, chert, sandstone, or granite
Support: Fracturing or abrading silica-bearing stone generates respirable crystalline silica dust linked to silicosis and lung disease.
Limitations: Outdoor breeze does not eliminate breathing-zone dust; avoid dry grinding/crushing and use eye and respiratory protection in authorized instruction. · Conflicting evidence: None noted.
Editorial note: Mandatory Z08 silica/eye safety gate. · Specialist reviewer: Industrial hygienist and geologist
Primary source · checked 2026-11-25
R08 · National Park Service / eCFR · 36 CFR 2.1 Preservation of Natural, Cultural and Archeological Resources (Current CFR)
Section: 36 CFR 2.1 natural resource collecting prohibitions · Conditions: National Parks, Monuments, and federal recreation areas
Support: Collecting rocks, minerals, fossils, wood, or natural specimens is prohibited on National Park System lands unless specifically authorized.
Limitations: Rules vary across USFS, BLM, state, tribal, county, and private lands; check the governing land manager before taking any sample. · Conflicting evidence: None noted.
Editorial note: Prevents unlawful federal resource extraction. · Specialist reviewer: Public-lands resource manager
