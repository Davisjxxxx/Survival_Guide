Z03-P15 · Fire and ignition · Adapted historical material

Separate dry deadwood interior from wet or rotted bark

Test fuel dryness inside the wood rather than judging by outer bark appearance after rain or snow.

When it applies · Preparing lawful dead and downed wood for an authorized outdoor fire in damp or winter conditions.

Equipment · Lawfully gathered dead downed wood, hand inspection and safe cutting tool if equipped and trained.

Actions

  1. Reject spongy, crumbling, punky or water-logged ground wood that bends or dampens to the touch—wet rot steams and extinguishes weak flames.[C03, C17, ED]
  2. Check dead small twigs and split sound deadwood cores where lawful; rain and snow often wet only the outer bark while leaving the sound interior fiber dry.[C03, ED]
  3. Snap-test fine twigs cleanly (crisp snap versus dull damp bend) and store prepared dry interior material off wet ground before attempting ignition.[C03, ED]

Failure recognition · Gathering damp surface sticks off soggy moss and mistaking dry-looking outer bark for dry interior kindling.[C03, ED]

Verify · Confirm tinder and small kindling snap crisply, feel dry against skin and stay isolated from wet snow or soil.[C03, ED]

Stop or change course · If available wood is saturated throughout and dry material cannot be staged safely, stop spending energy on fire and shift to no-fire insulation.[C03, C08]
