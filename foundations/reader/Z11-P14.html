Z11-P14 · Navigation, weather and rescue · Adapted historical material

Reconcile true, grid and magnetic north before converting bearings

Prevent systematic off-target navigation drift by distinguishing the three north references and applying current local declination accurately.

When it applies · Converting bearings between topographic maps and magnetic compasses in any region.

Equipment · Current topographic map, baseplate compass and current-year NOAA local declination value.

Actions

  1. Distinguish the three norths: True North points to the geographic North Pole, Grid North follows UTM map grid lines (usually close to true north), and Magnetic North points toward the shifting magnetic pole read by your compass needle.[T08, N08]
  2. Look up the current local magnetic declination (east or west angle) for your exact destination and year before leaving home—declination drifts every year, making old map margins inaccurate.[T08, N01]
  3. Use one consistent conversion method (adjustable-declination compass set once pre-trip, or written map-to-field conversion rule verified on a known landmark) and double-check sign direction (East vs West) before walking.[T08, N08, ED]

Failure recognition · Adding an East declination when it should have been subtracted (doubling the error) or using a 1965 sheet's magnetic diagram unchanged today.[T08, N01, N08]

Verify · Test your adjusted compass on a known straight road or map-aligned trail at the trailhead to confirm the reading matches expected bearing.[T08, N08, ED]

Stop or change course · If you are unsure whether to add or subtract declination in the field, pause on known ground and rely on verified trail handrails until the conversion is confirmed.[T08, N08, ED]

N01 · USGS · US Topo Maps for America (current web edition)
Section: US Topo Maps > Coordinate Grids, Scale and Currency · Conditions: Map reading and trip planning in the United States
Support: USGS topographic maps provide elevation contours, hydrography, roads, land cover and scale/date metadata; check publication date because trails, roads and structures change over time.
Limitations: Historical topo sheets (such as 1965 Ely quadrangle) illustrate symbols only and never establish current routes or declination. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with USGS-MAP-R and NOAA-D.

N08 · NOAA NCEI · Magnetic Declination (current web edition)
Section: Magnetic Declination > Local Anomalies and Compass Interference · Conditions: Using magnetic baseplate compasses near metal, electronics or iron-rich rock
Support: True north, grid north and magnetic north differ by local declination; additionally, nearby phones, vehicles, knives, steel poles and localized magnetic rock (e.g., northern Minnesota iron formations) deflect compass needles erratically.
Limitations: Never assume a compass needle reads true magnetic field while held near gear, vehicles or iron ore. · Conflicting evidence: Aligns with B014-P13 and B014-P14.
Editorial note: Grounds declination conversion, metal-clearance and magnetic-anomaly cards. · Specialist reviewer: Navigation and geophysics safety
