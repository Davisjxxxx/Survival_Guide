Z10-P43 · Wilderness medicine · Adapted historical material

Stabilize cold-water immersion survivors and guard against post-rescue collapse

Protect a person pulled from icy or cold water from silent post-exit cooling, shock, and sudden collapse.

When it applies · Anyone pulled from cold lakes, rivers, ice breakthroughs, or swamped watercraft—even if initially talking.

Equipment · Windproof shelter or heated building/vehicle, thick ground pad, dry blankets/layers, and 911 dispatch contact.

Actions

  1. Call 911 after significant cold immersion, breathing trouble or altered alertness. Once the person is safely out of the water, shelter them from wind, handle them gently and follow the dispatcher on position and movement.[M23, M26, T03]
  2. Remove soaked clothing gently (cutting it off if movement causes strain), pat skin dry without vigorous rubbing, insulate heavily underneath and around the torso and head, and monitor breathing and mental clarity continuously.[M23, E10]
  3. Watch closely for delayed deterioration ("circum-rescue collapse" or continued core cooling) and potential water aspiration even if the person initially insists they feel fine.[X02, M23, M05]

Failure recognition · Letting someone who fell through lake ice walk a mile back to a cabin in wet clothes because they initially feel adrenaline-warm.[X02, M23]

Verify · Confirm survivor is horizontal, dry-wrapped out of wind, monitored continuously, and medically evaluated by EMS.[M23, T02]

Stop or change course · Any shivering cessation, confusion, coughing/breathing labor, or faintness escalates immediately to emergency resuscitation readiness and urgent transport.[M23, M03]

Section: Figure 2 Adult Basic Life Support Algorithm for Lay Rescuers; 6.1 Recognition of Cardiac Arrest · Conditions: Unresponsive adult with absent or abnormal breathing; early dispatch and AED access
Support: Recognize gasps as abnormal, activate EMS, start CPR and use an AED promptly. Follow prompts and resume after analysis or shock.

M05 · NIH / MedlinePlus · Shock (current web edition)
Section: Recognizing Circulatory Shock and Positioning · Conditions: Injured or ill patient with pale cool clammy skin, rapid weak pulse, confusion, or dizziness
Support: Treat suspected shock as a life-threatening emergency: call 911, control external bleeding, keep the person warm and insulated from cold ground, and do not give food or drink.
Limitations: Field responders cannot diagnose internal hemorrhage or administer IV fluids. · Conflicting evidence: None; reinforces keeping suspected shock patients NPO and warm.
Editorial note: Core shock recognition and stabilization boundary in Z10. · Specialist reviewer: Emergency and wilderness medicine

Limitations: No fixed lay delay before calling or following dispatch on CPR. Separate expert protocols require temperature, pulse and training context. · Conflicting evidence: See A02 claim audit for boundaries.

Limitations: Shivering may stop as hypothermia worsens; absence of shivering is not reassurance. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with WMS cold guidelines.

X02 · Minnesota DNR · Cold Water Safety (current web edition)
Section: Cold Water Safety > Cold Shock, Swim Failure and Hypothermia · Conditions: Immersion in water below 70°F (especially below 50°F / winter ice water)
Support: Cold shock can cause involuntary gasping and rapid breathing. Grip and swimming may deteriorate rapidly; flotation and early rescue matter. Onset and severity vary.
Limitations: Strong swimmers drown rapidly from cold shock and muscle incapacitation without flotation. · Conflicting evidence: Refutes myth that hypothermia is the only immediate cold-water killer.
Editorial note: Core physiological evidence for Z12 ice-breakthrough and maritime immersion cards. · Specialist reviewer: Cold-water rescue and wilderness medicine
