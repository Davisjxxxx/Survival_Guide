Z15-P25 · Disaster scenarios · New manuscript

Seek safe elevated refuge if sudden flash floodwater traps roads or homes

Make life-saving vertical-refuge decisions when evacuation routes are already cut off—without trapping occupants inside closed attics.

When it applies · Sudden urban or valley flash flooding where water surrounds a building or rises rapidly indoors.

Equipment · Phone for 911 rescue, whistle/bright cloth for roof signaling, and strict closed-attic avoidance.

Actions

  1. If floodwater surrounds your home and safe dry evacuation is NO LONGER POSSIBLE, move immediately to the HIGHEST OPEN LEVEL of the building—never stay in a basement or crawlspace where rising water and electrical hazards trap people rapidly.[D05]
  2. NEVER climb into a closed, windowless attic where rising floodwater can trap you beneath a solid roof with no escape—climb onto the roof ONLY if rising water forces you upward as a last resort, call 911 with your exact address, and signal clearly for water/air rescue.[D05, T02]
  3. Do NOT enter rushing outdoor floodwater trying to swim or wade to safety—wait for trained swift-water rescue teams unless the structure itself is actively collapsing.[D05, X02]

Failure recognition · Retreating into a sealed garage attic with no exterior access hatch as flash floodwater rises past the second floor.[D05]

Verify · Confirm household knows highest open-access refuge level, avoids closed windowless attics, and calls 911 early if surrounded.[D05]

Stop or change course · Rising water approaching basement electrical panels or lower exits requires moving upward early—never waiting in basements to "save boxes."[D05, U01]

Section: Floods: During; Turn Around, Don’t Drown · Conditions: Flooded roads or trails, flood alert, or post-flood re-entry
Support: Move to safe higher ground when directed, never walk or drive into floodwater, and respect closures and re-entry restrictions.
Limitations: Depth examples are warning illustrations, never safe entry thresholds. Trapped or submerged vehicles require emergency help and scene-specific response. · Conflicting evidence: See A02 claim audit for boundaries.

U01 · FEMA / Ready.gov · Power Outages (current web edition)
Section: Power Outages > Preparing and Responding Safely · Conditions: Residential utility electrical outages of hours to days
Support: Keep phones/medical batteries charged, maintain flashlights instead of candles, unplug sensitive appliances to protect against restoration surges, and verify backup power plans for electrically dependent medical devices.
Limitations: Home outage durations vary widely by grid damage; extended winter outages require heat/evacuation contingencies. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with CPSC and NFPA.
Editorial note: Establishes household outage baseline and medical-power priority in Z13. · Specialist reviewer: Emergency management and electrical safety
Primary source · checked 2026-11-24
X02 · Minnesota DNR · Cold Water Safety (current web edition)
Section: Cold Water Safety > Cold Shock, Swim Failure and Hypothermia · Conditions: Immersion in water below 70°F (especially below 50°F / winter ice water)
Support: Cold shock can cause involuntary gasping and rapid breathing. Grip and swimming may deteriorate rapidly; flotation and early rescue matter. Onset and severity vary.
Limitations: Strong swimmers drown rapidly from cold shock and muscle incapacitation without flotation. · Conflicting evidence: Refutes myth that hypothermia is the only immediate cold-water killer.
Editorial note: Core physiological evidence for Z12 ice-breakthrough and maritime immersion cards. · Specialist reviewer: Cold-water rescue and wilderness medicine
