Z10-P28 · Wilderness medicine · New manuscript

Prevent and protect friction blisters and foot-skin breakdown

Catch footwear friction hot spots before skin tears and recognize high-risk foot infections that halt backcountry travel.

When it applies · Multi-day hiking, wet-trail portaging, snowshoeing, or heavy-pack travel.

Equipment · Dry spare wool/synthetic socks, breathable blister tape/moleskin, clean gauze, and potable water.

Actions

  1. Stop immediately at the very first warm stinging "hot spot" on a heel or toe—air and dry the foot, change into dry socks, remove grit from boots, and place smooth friction-reducing tape or donut padding around the spot.[M21, E10]
  2. Keep intact fluid-filled blisters unbroken whenever possible because intact roof skin is the body's best sterile dressing; if a blister tears open on its own, rinse gently with clean potable water, leave loose skin flat if clean, and cover with sterile non-stick dressing.[M21, M06, W29]
  3. Treat ANY foot wound, blister, or skin crack in a person with diabetes, neuropathy, or poor circulation as a high-risk medical issue requiring strict protection and early clinical inspection.[M21]

Failure recognition · Ignoring a burning heel hot spot for five miles until raw torn skin fills with trail mud and becomes infected.[M21, M06]

Verify · Confirm feet are inspected dry at every camp stop and no spreading redness, pus, or worsening limp is developing.[M21, M06]

Stop or change course · Spreading redness, warmth, pus, inability to walk normally, or any foot breakdown in a diabetic traveler requires halting the trip and seeking clinical evaluation.[M21, M06]
