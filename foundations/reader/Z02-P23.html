Z02-P23 · Shelter and housing · New manuscript

Separate conductive, convective, radiative and evaporative heat loss

Target each distinct physical heat-loss pathway with the right barrier instead of relying on a single layer.

When it applies · Designing and troubleshooting cold-weather clothing, bedding and shelter systems in any environment.

Equipment · Ground insulation pad, windproof outer barrier, dry insulating loft and vapor-management clothing layers.

Actions

  1. Block conduction into cold ground or snow first using dry uncompressed insulation beneath the body; air and ground drain heat rapidly through compressed fabric.[E05, E20]
  2. Block convection with a windbreak or windproof shell, and limit evaporation by shedding damp cotton, venting sweat during work and keeping rain off insulation.[E15, E10, E05]
  3. Recognize that radiation still escapes to clear night skies and cold walls; overhead cover and dry lofted layers slow radiative cooling without sealing away fresh breathing air.[E05, E15, ED]

Failure recognition · Wrapping in a windproof jacket while lying directly on frozen soil, or piling on thick wool while standing exposed in driving wind.[E05, E15, E10]

Verify · Point to the exact barrier addressing ground contact, moving air, wetness and sky exposure in your current setup.[E05, E15, E10, ED]

Stop or change course · If shivering becomes uncontrollable, speech slurs or hands fumble despite barriers, stop adjusting gear and initiate hypothermia escalation and emergency help.[E06, T03]

E15 · NWS · Cold Weather Safety (current web edition)
Section: Cold Weather Safety > Wind Chill and Dressing for Cold · Conditions: Cold, windy conditions where exposed skin cools rapidly
Support: Wind removes the thin warm air boundary layer around skin and permeable fabrics, sharply accelerating heat loss and frostbite onset; windproof outer shells and sheltered microclimates mitigate convective loss.
Limitations: Wind chill charts apply to exposed skin and do not change freezing point of water or equipment. · Conflicting evidence: None; clarifies physical mechanism of convective heat loss.
Editorial note: Supports wind-block geometry, shell layering, and wind-chill risk decisions. · Specialist reviewer: Cold-weather shelter and meteorology
