Z04-P27 · Water · Adapted historical material

Melt snow safely without scorching vessels or wasting fuel

Convert raw snow into liquid water efficiently in a heat-safe pot while keeping melting distinct from microbial treatment.

When it applies · Cold-weather camp with a rated outdoor stove or permitted fire and a suitable metal cooking vessel.

Equipment · Heat-safe metal pot with lid, rated outdoor stove and fuel, starter liquid water if available and sanitized finished container.

Actions

  1. Start with a small layer of liquid water in the bottom of the pot if available so heat conducts evenly and snow does not vaporize and scorch the dry metal.[W02, W26, ED]
  2. Add snow gradually in dense handfuls with the lid on to reduce wind cooling and fuel consumption, operating the stove strictly outdoors away from shelters.[C05, C15, W26, ED]
  3. Once melted, clarify any needles or grit if needed and then bring the water to a full rolling boil (1 minute; 3 minutes above 6,500 feet) or apply another validated treatment.[W02, W08]

Failure recognition · Packing fluffy dry snow into a dry pot over high flame—burning the metal—or drinking freshly melted snow without boiling or disinfecting.[W02, W09, ED]

Verify · Liquid meltwater completes a timed rolling boil or validated treatment cycle and transfers into a clean covered vessel.[W02, W06, ED]

Stop or change course · Never melt snow with a fuel stove inside a tent, vestibule or vehicle; stop and switch to carried reserves if fuel or safe outdoor setup is insufficient.[C05, C16, W26]
