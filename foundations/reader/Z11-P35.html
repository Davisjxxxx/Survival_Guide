Z11-P35 · Navigation, weather and rescue · New manuscript

Cross-check two visible landmark bearings for an approximate position fix

Estimate position on open terrain by intersecting bearings from two or three clearly identified mapped landmarks.

When it applies · Open ridges, lakeshores or clearings with unambiguous sightlines to two or three distinct mapped features.

Equipment · Current topographic map, baseplate compass, current declination and pencil.

Actions

  1. Use unmistakable mapped landmarks in substantially different directions. Near-parallel bearings make an approximate intersection less useful.[N03, ED]
  2. Measure each field-to-map bearing clear of metal, apply current declination cleanly and draw or project each line backward from its mapped landmark across the sheet.[T08, N08]
  3. Read the intersection (or small triangle of uncertainty from three bearings) together with local contour elevation and slope direction—real field fixes are approximate zones, not pinpoint dots.[N03, N08, ED]

Failure recognition · Misidentifying the wrong mountain peak or lake bay and trusting a bogus intersection over obvious ground contours.[N03, N08]

Verify · Confirm that your current elevation contour, ground slope and nearby vegetation match the intersection zone on the map.[N03, ED]

Stop or change course · If the plotted triangle is huge or disagrees with surrounding ground relief, reject the fix, re-verify landmark IDs and avoid stepping into uncertain terrain.[N03, T06]

N03 · USGS · What is a Topographic Map (current web edition)
Section: What is a Topographic Map > Contours and Relief · Conditions: Matching three-dimensional terrain shapes to two-dimensional contour lines
Support: Closed contour rings denote peaks or depressions (hachured), V-patterns point uphill in valleys/drainages and wide spacing marks gentle slopes while tight spacing marks cliffs or steep grades.
Limitations: Dense forest canopy or snow drifts can obscure subtle terrain features shown on the map. · Conflicting evidence: None; core topographic terrain-association evidence.
Editorial note: Supports ridge, valley, saddle and handrail terrain-interpretation cards. · Specialist reviewer: Navigation and cartography

N08 · NOAA NCEI · Magnetic Declination (current web edition)
Section: NOAA Magnetic Declination > definition and true-bearing conversion · Conditions: Using magnetic baseplate compasses near metal, electronics or iron-rich rock
Support: Declination is between true and magnetic north and varies by location and time. Grid convergence is separate. A device may already apply a correction.
Limitations: NOAA definition does not establish site-specific interference distances or diagnosis of magnetic anomalies. · Conflicting evidence: Aligns with B014-P13 and B014-P14.
Editorial note: Grounds declination conversion, metal-clearance and magnetic-anomaly cards. · Specialist reviewer: Navigation and geophysics safety
