Z02-P29 · Shelter and housing · Adapted historical material

Avoid cotton and manage damp outer shells inside sleeping areas

Keep moisture-holding fabrics off skin and prevent wet outer rainwear or snow-dusted gear from soaking dry sleep insulation.

When it applies · Cool, wet, windy or freezing trips where rain, snowmelt or sweat wets outer clothing.

Equipment · Synthetic or wool base/mid layers, waterproof breathable or wind shell, dedicated dry sleep bag and vestibule or pack cover.

Actions

  1. Leave cotton shirts, jeans and cotton socks out of cold/wet field kits—cotton absorbs water readily, collapses thermally and accelerates cooling against skin.[E10, E06]
  2. Shake snow and droplets off outer shells before entering shelter and isolate wet boots, rain jackets and wet gloves in a vestibule or waterproof bag apart from dry bedding.[E10, E05, ED]
  3. Change out of damp daytime base layers only after entering wind-protected shelter with dry replacement garments immediately in hand.[E05, E10]

Failure recognition · Climbing straight into a sleeping bag wearing damp cotton pants or leaving snow-caked jackets piled on top of sleeping bags inside a small tent.[E10, E06]

Verify · Confirm skin-contact sleep layers are dry wool or synthetic and wet outer items stay physically segregated from sleeping insulation.[E10, ED]

Stop or change course · If a companion cannot warm up after moving out of wind and changing into dry layers, escalate for suspected hypothermia immediately.[E06, T03]
