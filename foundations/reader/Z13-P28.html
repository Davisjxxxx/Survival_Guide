Z13-P28 · Home survival and utilities · New manuscript

Operate the main residential water shutoff valve to limit burst-pipe flooding

Stop pressurized indoor water flooding within seconds if a pipe splits during a freeze-thaw event or plumbing failure.

When it applies · Every house, townhome, or cabin with municipal water or a pressurized private well tank.

Equipment · Tagged main shutoff valve (ball valve or gate valve), adjustable wrench or curb-key if applicable, and flashlight.

Actions

  1. Identify the exact main water shutoff valve (typically where the supply line enters the basement/crawlspace on the street or well side, plus the well pump breaker for private wells) and tag it with a bright waterproof label.[U08, U14, U06]
  2. Know your valve type gently: a quarter-turn lever ball valve closes perpendicular (90°) to the pipe, while a round wheel gate valve closes clockwise ("righty-tighty")—never force a seized old gate valve with excessive cheater-bar leverage that snaps the stem.[U08, ED]
  3. If a pipe bursts, shut off the main water valve immediately (and switch off the well pump breaker on private wells), turn off power to any electric water heater so dry elements do not burn out, and open the lowest faucet to relieve residual pressure.[U08, U10]

Failure recognition · Watching hundreds of gallons of pressurized water cascade through ceiling drywall into light fixtures while hunting for an untagged shutoff valve.[U08, U14]

Verify · Confirm valve location is cleared of stored boxes, tagged brightly, and every adult knows whether it turns 90° or clockwise.[U08, U14]

Stop or change course · Water flooding near electrical outlets, breakers, or appliances requires shutting off electricity safely from a dry zone (or exiting and calling the utility/fire department) before wading in.[U08, D05]
