SNOW CAN HIDE THE HOLE

A white cover hides cracks, slush, overflow and thickness changes.

HOW THE DECISION OR ACTION WORKS

  1. Evaluate from land — Identify slush bands, dark/wet patches, overflow and concealed stream outlets.
  2. Recognize insulation — Snow can slow ice growth. Heavy snow can also load the ice.
  3. Separate snow from ice — Refrozen surface snow (white ice) is not equivalent to new clear ice.
  4. Select a dry-land alternative — If the layer beneath cannot be inspected and measured, do not travel across.

CHECK BEFORE CONTINUING

  • No one uses a pole poke as proof of safe travel.
  • Snow-covered drainage routes and culverts are treated as hazards.
  • No numeric ice-strength claim is inferred from appearance.

WRONG SIGNAL → CORRECTION

It held yesterdayConditions and snow loading can change rapidly.
Snow crust is hardA strong crust does not measure ice underneath.
Puddles are shallowOverflow/slush may hide weak ice and openings.
STOP CONDITION: Hidden ice condition, slush, overflow, current or snow cover without measurement: remain off.
