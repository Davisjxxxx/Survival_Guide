NO-GEAR WINTER DETOUR

Worst-case response when you cannot verify the ice

STAGED SCENE • NOT A RELIABLE ICE-CONDITION IDENTIFICATION PHOTO
A stick, footprints, an animal track or a frozen shoreline is not a valid substitute for measured ice conditions.

HOW THE DECISION OR ACTION WORKS

  1. Choose safe ground — Look for a route around the waterbody, not across it.
  2. Check the detour — Avoid steep banks, unstable snow, open channels and hidden marsh water.
  3. Limit unnecessary travel — If the detour increases exposure beyond your capacity, find shelter and make yourself visible.
  4. Signal and document — Use a phone or high-contrast ground marker when available.

CHECK BEFORE CONTINUING

  • All water crossings remain excluded.
  • The group can describe its next safe decision.

WRONG SIGNAL → CORRECTION

Try breaking thin ice with a branchNo improvised probing method establishes safe load capacity.
Follow a snowmobile trailPrevious users may have had knowledge or equipment you lack.
Keep moving while lostEscalate to shelter and rescue instead of compounding exposure.
STOP CONDITION: If ice strength cannot be verified, stay off. If someone has fallen through, summon emergency responders immediately.
