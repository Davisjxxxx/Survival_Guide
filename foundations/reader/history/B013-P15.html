B013 · MINNESOTA ICE SAFETY · P15 · RECREATION / FISHING DECISIONS

FISHING IS NEVER THE FIRST PRIORITY

If the fishing spot requires risky ice travel, abandon the fishing plan.

ORIGINAL SAFETY SCHEMATIC · NOT A SITE PHOTOGRAPH
No fish harvest can offset an uncontrolled immersion hazard. If you cannot establish safe access, do not enter the ice. Long stationary loads have separate DNR guidelines. A walking measurement cannot clear a fishing site.

FIELD ACTION · NO GEAR FIRST

1
No equipment: choose landKeep yourself and companions on stable ground. Find a safe land detour or leave the area.
2
Improvised: do not improvise accessOnly consider ordinary shore fishing at legally accessible open water, when conditions permit. Do not use improvised ice holes or traps.
3
Equipped: decide before travelingCheck the local lake and current DNR rules, tell a contact your plan, bring flotation and rescue equipment, and measure ice repeatedly.
4
Stop when conditions changeSnow, wind, current, spring deterioration and water near structures can invalidate a prior observation.

VISUAL DECISION / METHOD

  1. No equipment: choose land — Keep yourself and companions on stable ground. Find a safe land detour or leave the area.
  2. Improvised: do not improvise access — Only consider ordinary shore fishing at legally accessible open water, when conditions permit. Do not use improvised ice holes or traps.
  3. Equipped: decide before traveling — Check the local lake and current DNR rules, tell a contact your plan, bring flotation and rescue equipment, and measure ice repeatedly.
  4. Stop when conditions change — Snow, wind, current, spring deterioration and water near structures can invalidate a prior observation.

CHECK BEFORE CONTINUING

  • Fishing is optional; warmth, shelter, water and signaling take priority in an emergency.
  • Have a current angling license or an applicable exemption, and check lake-specific rules.
  • No part of this guide certifies ice travel.

WRONG SIGNAL → CORRECTION

Tracks or anglers nearbyPrior use is not evidence that present ice is safe.
Four inches somewhereThe DNR threshold refers to NEW, CLEAR ice, not a blanket route approval.
Fish are visibleAbandon the route if access is uncertain.
STOP: No verified safe route, inadequate equipment, unknown ice, or unstable weather means NO ICE ENTRY.
