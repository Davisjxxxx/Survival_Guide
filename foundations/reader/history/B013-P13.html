B013 · MINNESOTA ICE SAFETY · P13 · 911 / LOCATION

CALL EARLY. BE EASY TO FIND.

Reliable location and access information can be as important as rescue equipment.

STAGED ILLUSTRATION • NOT A FIELD-VERIFIED ICE OR RESCUE PHOTOGRAPH
If someone breaks through or is trapped, call 911 immediately from a safe location.

HOW THE DECISION OR ACTION WORKS

  1. Call 911 — Report ice immersion or stranded people and say whether anyone is still in the water.
  2. Give a precise location — Lake or river name, access/road name, landmarks and GPS coordinates if available.
  3. Report what responders need — Number of people, observable condition, openings/currents and where crews can access land.
  4. Stay accessible — Follow dispatcher directions, keep your phone warm, and use visible signals from safe ground.

CHECK BEFORE CONTINUING

  • Dispatcher has your location and the best land access.
  • Group stays out of weak-ice rescue zones.
  • Backup signaling is considered if phone service fails.

WRONG SIGNAL → CORRECTION

“We are on a lake”Provide a named access point and distinct landmark.
No cellular signalUse a carried PLB/SOS device if available; signal from shore.
Call ends earlyStay available for callbacks where possible.
STOP CONDITION: Do not delay calling while searching for gear or attempting an ice-edge rescue.
