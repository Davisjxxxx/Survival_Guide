IF EQUIPPED: CHECK FIRST

Minnesota DNR minimums are screening guidance, not a guarantee

STAGED SCENE • NOT A RELIABLE ICE-CONDITION IDENTIFICATION PHOTO
Minnesota DNR says to consider on-foot travel only after measuring at least 4 in (10.16 cm) of new, clear ice — and even then ice is never guaranteed safe.

HOW THE DECISION OR ACTION WORKS

  1. Pre-plan with another person — Share route, time and emergency plan. Avoid solitary travel.
  2. Wear foam flotation and carry ice picks — Keep rescue tools immediately accessible.
  3. Drill or chisel a hole; measure — Use a tape to measure actual clear ice thickness, not surface snow.
  4. Recheck along the route — Conditions change; reject current, white ice, thaw or unmeasurable locations.

CHECK BEFORE CONTINUING

  • Measurement is repeated; no single spot stands for the lake.
  • A local expert and route-specific hazards have been checked.

WRONG SIGNAL → CORRECTION

A chisel did not break throughResistance is not a substitute for tape measurement.
4 inches measured onceNot proof that the entire route meets the guidance.
Forgot flotation or picksReturn for proper equipment; do not improvise safety.
STOP CONDITION: If ice strength cannot be verified, stay off. If someone has fallen through, summon emergency responders immediately.
