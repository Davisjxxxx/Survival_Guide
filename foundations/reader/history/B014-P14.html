TWO NORTHS, ONE UNVERIFIED NUMBER

A compass reading and map bearing can differ because of magnetic declination.

SCENARIO · A compass needle aligns to local magnetic north while this example uses true north. Check your map legend for its actual north reference.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: Without a reliable direction, do not guess a compass-equivalent heading.
IMPROVISED: Write down the last known position and reference points. Improvised magnetic needles are not a reliable emergency substitute.
EQUIPPED: Use an appropriate map, an understood compass and a current local NOAA declination value.
Four-action sequence
  1. IDENTIFY NORTH — Read the map legend and the compass reference before taking a bearing.
  2. LOOK UP — Obtain the magnetic declination for that location and date from a verified source.
  3. CHECK METHOD — Use the compass manufacturer’s procedure to convert map and magnetic bearings.
  4. GROUND-TRUTH — Confirm the intended path against reliable terrain features before moving.

Check before continuing

  • Is the declination value location- and date-specific?
  • Are you distinguishing true north and magnetic north?
  • Do observable features agree with your map?

Wrong signal → correction

Copy a bearing from an old guide → Declination changes with time and geography.
Use one compass reading → Local interference or incorrect technique can mislead.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: Unknown declination, unverified map orientation or inconsistent features: do not follow a calculated bearing.
