NO-EQUIPMENT PRIORITY

BUILD A DRY INSULATED BED.

Before a complicated shelter, separate your body from the frozen ground with material that keeps its loft.

Staged six-step image series. The sleeping bag in some frames represents the equipped upgrade.
Do this — no extra equipment
  1. Gather dead, dry leaves, needles, bark fragments, and sound fallen branches if available.
  2. Use stable, reasonably level ground; do not build over running water or unsafe ice.
  3. Arrange a stable base and generous dry loft above it.
  4. Press the bed with your body weight; correct compressed or wet spots.
  5. Add wind cover above the bed when possible, without an unstable overhead load.
VERIFY
The bed stays dry and separates the body from frozen ground under load.
FAILURE
Only a thin layer remains after compression; snow or seepage reaches bedding.
CORRECT
Add dry insulating layers or relocate to a better-drained site.
STOP: If there are no dry materials or cold injury is progressing, do not assume the bed is sufficient; seek safer shelter or rescue.UPGRADE: Salvaged dry fabric, emergency blanket used correctly, sleeping mat and bag.
