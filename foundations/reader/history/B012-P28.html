Winter Emergency: One-Page Action Map

A compact field decision sequence for lost, exposed or stranded people in Minnesota winter.

AT A GLANCE

  • First address immediate danger and cold injury.
  • Shelter, dryness, ground insulation, and rescue outrank optional food gathering.
  • Do not improvise unsafe ice travel or enclosed fires.
STOP → PROTECT → CONTACT → MONITOR
1

STOP and assess

Check injuries, mental status, hazards, last-known location, wind and wetness.

2

STAY WARM and dry

Use existing safe shelter, block wind, add dry insulation under body and protect extremities.

3

CALL and signal

Contact 911 or rescue if able; share location, stay visible and conserve battery.

4

RECHECK often

Monitor alertness, shivering, wetness, shelter stability, water needs and rescuers.

DECISION DIAGRAM
  1. STOP: assess injuries, cold and terrain danger.
  2. PROTECT: shelter, dry layers and insulation under the body.
  3. CALL and SIGNAL: emergency services, location and visibility.
  4. MONITOR: reassess alertness, shelter safety and new hazards.
NO EQUIPMENT → IMPROVISED → EQUIPPED

NO EQUIPMENT

Use terrain, sound deadfall windbreak, dry ground layer and deliberate ground contrast.

IMPROVISED

Use spare clothing, bags, blankets, reflective items, found containers and simple signaling gear.

EQUIPPED

Use emergency communication, shelter kit, rated insulation and water treatment; do not replace judgment with equipment.

IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
Travel seems faster than waitingIf route/ice/weather is uncertain, stay in a safe known place and signal.
Fire is not catchingStop repeated exertion and sweating; prioritize warmth and dryness.
Person becomes confused or less responsiveCall 911; this overrides ordinary field tasks.
