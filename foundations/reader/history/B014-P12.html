THE WATER BARRIER IS NOT A SHORTCUT

A lake, river, beaver pond or bog can interrupt a planned route.

SCENARIO · Your remembered trail appears to lead toward a frozen lake and an unmarked marsh.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: Remain on stable ground; do not test ice strength or mud depth with your body.
IMPROVISED: Mark your own safe stationary location with visible materials. Do not build unverified bridges or rafts.
EQUIPPED: Use current topographic maps and an official trail route; verify them against actual terrain.
Four-action sequence
  1. STOP SHORT — Observe from stable ground, away from unstable banks and shore ice.
  2. READ TERRAIN — Use known map features and visible landmarks to identify water and wetland boundaries.
  3. REJECT RISK — Do not cross uncertain ice, flooded ground or bog to shorten a route.
  4. DECIDE — Choose only a verified safe land detour. Otherwise stay in a safe place and request help.

Check before continuing

  • Is the route an authorized land route, not a water crossing?
  • Are shoreline and bank conditions observed from safe ground?
  • Does the map match the actual landmarks?

Wrong signal → correction

White surface looks walkable → Snow can conceal weak ice and open water.
A blue patch is easy to detour → Wetlands may be larger or less passable than a schematic suggests.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: Unknown ice, deep mud, moving water, unstable shoreline or no verified detour: do not enter.
