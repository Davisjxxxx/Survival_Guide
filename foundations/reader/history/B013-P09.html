THAWED ICE: NO SHORTCUT

Spring warmth, water and wind can destroy load capacity long before the lake looks open.

STAGED ILLUSTRATION • NOT A FIELD-VERIFIED ICE OR RESCUE PHOTOGRAPH
Treat thawing, waterlogged or seasonally deteriorating ice as impassable.

HOW THE DECISION OR ACTION WORKS

  1. Stop at stable land — Do not walk out to inspect the ice edge. Shorelines may be undercut or detached.
  2. Scan from shore — Look for open seams, water on ice, soft edges, movement and wind-driven breakup.
  3. Reject the spring crossing — Old or partially thawed ice can fail despite considerable apparent thickness.
  4. Change the route — Detour on land, shelter if necessary, or call for help rather than test it with your body.

CHECK BEFORE CONTINUING

  • Everyone remains off the ice and away from weak banks.
  • Route does not depend on a thin shore connection.
  • Wind, rain and daytime thaw are considered.

WRONG SIGNAL → CORRECTION

Surface looks frozenRecent thaw/refreeze is not evidence of underlying strength.
Tracks lead acrossEarlier crossings are not verification.
Open water near bankDo not approach an overhanging or detached edge.
STOP CONDITION: Spring deterioration, open water or thaw/refreeze history: do not enter. Choose land access.
