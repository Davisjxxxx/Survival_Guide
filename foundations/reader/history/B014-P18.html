THE LAST LIGHT DECISION

Daylight and weather may run out before an uncertain detour ends.

SCENARIO · The trail is unclear and visibility is decreasing before nightfall.
Options from least equipment
NO GEAR: Stay out of wind and wet terrain. Avoid unnecessary exertion that increases sweat and heat loss.
IMPROVISED: Use safe natural shelter features and dry materials for wind protection and insulation from the ground.
EQUIPPED: Use a planned emergency shelter, dry layers, headlamp, food/water and an emergency communicator.
Four-action sequence
  1. ASSESS TIME — Estimate remaining usable daylight without assuming a known sunset time.
  2. COMPARE HAZARDS — Check whether travel or staying exposes you to greater immediate danger.
  3. PROTECT — Choose safer stable ground, block wind and preserve dry insulation.
  4. SIGNAL + WAIT — Notify responders when possible; remain findable and monitor everyone for cold stress.

Check before continuing

  • Is remaining in place protected from wind and wet ground?
  • Is anyone wet, confused, unusually sleepy or unable to walk safely?
  • Are responders aware of the likely overnight situation?

Wrong signal → correction

Push on until dark → Navigating uncertain obstacles without light compounds risk.
Sweat heavily to stay warm → Wet clothing increases cold exposure risk.
STOP / ABORT CONDITION: When darkness, uncertain route or cold injury changes the risk, stop discretionary travel and seek emergency help.
