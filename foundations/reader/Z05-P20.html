Red and White Clover (Trifolium pratense & T. repens) Profile

Document diagnostic traits of Trifolium pratense and Trifolium repens and contrasting lookalikes under permanent consumption HOLD.

When it applies · Fields, meadows, open trails; Spring-Autumn (Trifoliate leaves & globe heads).

Equipment · Regional references, accession information and non-contact photographs or notes, where safe.

Actions

  1. Compare the working name Trifolium pratense and Trifolium repens (L.; syn. Meadow clover) against a current regional key and accession. Unverified draft cues: Three-leaflet (trifoliate) leaves often marked with pale V-shaped chevron; dense rounded pink-magenta or white clover flower heads; stipules clasping stem.[B01, B03, B20]
  2. Use the comparisons and draft traits in the taxonomic register for qualified regional review. Do not use any single feature, comparison, smell or image to rule out a toxic species.[B02, B20, B24]
  3. Separate recognition from eating (Moldy clover produces dicoumarol anticoagulants; legume allergy or interaction cautions; consumption withheld); keep WITHHELD_HOLD and check site and species protections before any specialist fieldwork.[B19, B21, B24]

Failure recognition · Single-trait guessing or common names confuse Trifolium pratense and Trifolium repens with Oxalis, risking poisoning.[B03, B10, B24]

Verify · Keep the identification provisional until the voucher, region, life stage and independent diagnostic characters are reviewed by the qualified specialist.[B02, B20]

Stop or change course · Keep consumption on WITHHELD_HOLD. For suspected ingestion call U.S. Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222 promptly. Call 911 for collapse, seizure, breathing trouble or inability to wake.[B09, M15]
