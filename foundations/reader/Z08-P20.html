Iron Formations, Hematite, Magnetite and Compass Deflection

Recognize northern Minnesota iron-bearing formations and account for local magnetic compass interference during land navigation.

When it applies · Mesabi, Vermilion, and Cuyuna Iron Range regions and iron-rich glacial cobbles.

Equipment · Hand lens, baseplate compass, regional geologic map.

Actions

  1. Recognize banded iron formations and iron oxides by noticeably high density, metallic-to-earthy dark gray or brick-red bands, and reddish-brown hematite streak.[R01, R04, R20]
  2. Test for local magnetic anomalies by taking reciprocal compass sightings across short distances whenever traveling over iron-formation bedrock or mafic gabbro.[R20, N03]
  3. Keep clear of abandoned mine pits, tailings basins, and unstable rust-stained waste rock piles, which pose steep-drop and heavy-metal runoff hazards.[R11, R12, R14]

Failure recognition · Trusting a single magnetic bearing over magnetite bedrock steers travelers off course, while entering old mine tailings risks collapse and contaminant exposure.[R12, R14, R20]

Verify · Navigation cross-checks terrain features against compass readings and routes around mine pits and tailings.[R14, R20]

Stop or change course · Stop navigating solely by magnetic needle whenever needle behavior shifts between nearby standing points, and stay outside fenced or flooded mine pits.[R11, R20]
