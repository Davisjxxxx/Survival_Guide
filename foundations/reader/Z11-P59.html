Z11-P59 · Navigation, weather and rescue · New manuscript

Use VHF/FRS/GMRS short-range radios within realistic terrain line-of-sight limits

Coordinate camp or group movements accurately with handheld radios while understanding terrain blockage and licensing rules.

When it applies · Multi-person outdoor groups, basecamp-to-party check-ins and disaster neighborhood coordination using handheld two-way radios.

Equipment · Matched handheld radios (FRS or properly licensed GMRS/amateur/marine VHF per FCC rules), spare batteries and pre-agreed channel/schedule card.

Actions

  1. Understand line-of-sight physics: despite optimistic packaging range claims, handheld VHF/UHF radios communicate cleanly across open water or hilltops yet drop to under a mile through dense hills, valleys or buildings.[T11, T15, ED]
  2. Agree on one primary channel, one backup channel, privacy-code settings (knowing codes block incoming noise rather than providing privacy) and fixed hourly check-in windows to conserve batteries.[T11, E02, ED]
  3. Follow federal licensing rules strictly—FRS is license-free at low power, whereas GMRS, amateur radio and specific marine/aviation frequencies carry legal licensing and lawful-use requirements.[T11, X05, ED]

Failure recognition · Trusting a "35-mile range" blister-pack claim on cheap FRS walkie-talkies across two wooded mountain ridges and losing all contact with a split party.[T11, E11]

Verify · Test actual radio clarity between group members on the agreed channel before separating, and verify spare batteries stay warm and dry.[T11, E02, ED]

Stop or change course · Never assume anyone is monitoring random FRS/GMRS channels during an emergency—pair handheld radios with 911/PLB/written trip plans for real rescue escalation.[T11, N05, T06]

Section: SARSAT Beacons > PLB, EPIRB and Registration · Conditions: Emergency satellite distress signaling (406 MHz PLBs and EPIRBs)
Support: Registered 406 MHz Personal Locator Beacons transmit distress alerts via NOAA SARSAT satellites directly to rescue coordination centers with no subscription; registration with NOAA is legally required and must stay current.
Limitations: PLBs are one-way distress alerts only (no two-way texting), require clear sky view and must be reserved for grave and imminent danger. · Conflicting evidence: Distinguishes one-way 406 MHz PLBs from commercial two-way satellite messengers.
