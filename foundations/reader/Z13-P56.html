Z13-P56 · Home survival and utilities · New manuscript

Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms monthly and replace expired sensors

Maintain reliable early life-safety warning across every sleeping area and floor before winter heating or outage stressors begin.

When it applies · Every house, apartment, duplex, and cabin year-round.

Equipment · UL-listed smoke and CO alarms (interconnected or battery-backed), fresh backup batteries, and manufacture-date inspection calendar.

Actions

  1. Install working smoke alarms inside every bedroom, outside each sleeping area, and on every floor (including basements), AND working carbon monoxide alarms on every level and outside all sleeping areas strictly per manufacturer placement instructions.[C22, C09, U12, U04]
  2. Press the TEST button on every smoke and CO alarm monthly, replace removable backup batteries at least annually (or use sealed 10-year lithium-battery units), and vacuum dust gently from sensor vents.[U12, U04]
  3. Check the manufacture/expiration date printed on the back of EVERY unit—CO sensors typically expire after 5 to 7 years (up to 10 on newer sealed units) and smoke alarms expire after 10 years, losing sensing accuracy even if the test beep still chirps.[U12, C09]

Failure recognition · Assuming a 14-year-old yellowed CO alarm still detects lethal gas just because its test button makes noise.[U12, C09]

Verify · Confirm alarms present on every floor and outside bedrooms, monthly test logged, and every unit is within its manufacturer lifespan.[U12, U04]

Stop or change course · Any steady CO alarm (exit to fresh air + 911 immediately), smoke alarm with fire, or end-of-life chirp requires immediate safety action or same-day unit replacement.[C07, U12]

Support: Escape to fresh air immediately and contact emergency services. Symptoms can include headache, dizziness, nausea and confusion.

C22 · CPSC · Carbon Monoxide Questions and Answers (current web edition)
Section: Carbon Monoxide Questions and Answers: CO alarm installation and maintenance; fuel-burning equipment indoors · Conditions: Home CO alarms and improvised indoor heat
Support: Install battery-operated or battery-backup CO alarms on every level and outside sleeping areas, not in kitchens, above fuel-burning appliances or near heating vents; the test button checks circuitry, not sensor accuracy; alarms have a recommended replacement age from the manufacturer. Never use gas ranges, ovens or clothes dryers to heat a home; never burn charcoal inside a home, garage, vehicle or tent; never use portable fuel-burning camping equipment indoors unless it is designed for enclosed spaces.
Limitations: No fixed alarm lifespan; follow manufacturer instructions. · Conflicting evidence: The CO-alarm lifespan in years on Z13-P56 is not on this page.
Editorial note: Grounds Z13-P56 placement and the Z13-P20 indoor-heat prohibitions. · Specialist reviewer: Fire prevention and carbon monoxide safety

Section: Home Fires > Smoke Alarms, Fire Extinguishers, and Escape Plans · Conditions: Residential fire prevention during outages and supplemental heating
Support: Test smoke alarms monthly on every level and outside sleeping areas, keep two exits clear from every room, store fuels outside living spaces in approved containers, and evacuate immediately if a fire exceeds incipient containment.
Limitations: Candles and improvised indoor flames dramatically elevate outage house-fire deaths. · Conflicting evidence: None; integrates with CPSC CO-alarm rules (C09).

U12 · CPSC · CO Alarms (current web edition)
Section: Home Alarms > Smoke, Carbon Monoxide, and Battery Backup Readiness · Conditions: Occupied residences with fuel-burning furnaces, water heaters, attached garages, or outage generators
Support: Maintain interconnected or battery-backed CO and smoke alarms on every floor and outside sleeping rooms, replace expired sensors on schedule, and keep exterior furnace/dryer/exhaust vents clear of drifting snow.
Limitations: Blocked high-efficiency PVC furnace intake/exhaust pipes in heavy snow can force CO back indoors or lock out heating systems. · Conflicting evidence: None; integrates with C09 and Z13-R03.
