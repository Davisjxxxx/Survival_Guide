Z11-P61 · Navigation, weather and rescue · Adapted historical material

Deploy high-contrast ground markers and mirror flashes for air or ground search

Create unmistakable visual targets visible from aircraft or distant ridges without risking unsafe exposure or fire.

When it applies · Daylight waiting in open clearings, snowfields, shorelines or ridges when expecting search aircraft or ground teams.

Equipment · High-contrast orange/bright tarp or space blanket, rocks/branches for weighting and acrylic or glass signal mirror.

Actions

  1. Lay out large geometric contrast shapes (large X, V or long straight bright tarp strips weighted securely with rocks/snow) in an open clearing adjacent to your shelter—nature rarely forms sharp straight neon shapes.[N09, T06]
  2. On bright snow, stomp out large deep letters or dark evergreen-branch lines (using dead downed branches) to maximize shadow and color contrast from overhead aircraft.[N09, K11, ED]
  3. Use a sighting signal mirror strictly on sunny days to sweep brief flashes across distant ridges or toward searching aircraft—avoid blinding helicopter pilots continuously at close landing approach.[N09, ED]

Failure recognition · Waving a small gray handkerchief from beneath tall pine branches where aerial spotters cannot distinguish motion or color.[N09]

Verify · Confirm ground markers are large, high-contrast against the seasonal background, securely weighted against wind/rotor wash and visible from open sky.[N09, ED]

Stop or change course · Secure all loose tarps, jackets and gear tightly before any rescue helicopter approaches—loose fabric sucked into rotor blades endangers the entire aircrew.[N09, T01]
