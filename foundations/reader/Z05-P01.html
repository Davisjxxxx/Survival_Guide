Z05-P01 · Wild foods and poisonous lookalikes · New manuscript

Taxonomic Workflow: Separating Recognition from Consumption

Establish multi-character botanical and fungal identification gates while keeping all consumption decisions strictly withheld pending qualified expert review.

When it applies · All wild plant, berry, root, tree, and mushroom study across Minnesota and the Upper Midwest.

Equipment · Regional taxonomic key, Bell Museum Atlas reference, metric ruler, notebook, camera.

Actions

  1. Verify each candidate species by accepted Latin binomial, taxonomic authority, synonyms, and regional Bell Museum Minnesota Biodiversity Atlas occurrence rather than ambiguous common names.[B01, B02, B03]
  2. Confirm multiple independent morphological characters at the observed life stage and cross-check every regional poisonous lookalike before recording a provisional field identification.[B20, B24]
  3. Treat species recognition and suitability for consumption as completely separate decisions; generated diagrams, single photos, and unreviewed draft profiles never authorize eating wild species.[B10, B21, B24]

Failure recognition · Relying on common names, phone-app guesses, or single-photo matches causes lethal plant and mushroom poisonings.[B02, B06, B24]

Verify · Accepted binomial, multi-character traits, contrasting toxic lookalikes, and explicit consumption HOLD status are recorded together.[B03, B20, B24]

Stop or change course · Stop identification immediately if any required diagnostic structure is missing, damaged, or inconsistent; call 1-800-222-1222 for any suspected toxic ingestion.[B09, B20, B24]

Qualified review pending · Regional botanist, mycologist, and medical toxicologist
