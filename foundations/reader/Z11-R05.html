Z11-R05 · Navigation, weather and rescue · Reconstruction draft

Orient a paper map to terrain using a baseplate compass

Align the printed map with real surrounding landforms so left, right and ahead match the landscape.

Lineage · B014 reconstruction · r01 slot R05 (original B014-P05 missing)

When it applies · Trailheads, junctions and open viewpoints when matching map symbols to visible features; reconstructs missing B014-P05.

Equipment · Paper topographic map and baseplate compass kept clear of metal and electronics.

Actions

  1. Step clear of vehicles, steel signs, knives and phones, lay the baseplate edge along a north-south neat line, turn the dial until ring north agrees with map north, then correct for current local declination unless an adjustable compass is already set.[N14, N08, T08]
  2. Rotate the map and compass together flat in your hands until the magnetic needle aligns smoothly inside the orienting arrow—now map features point in the same direction as the real terrain.[N14, N03, ED]
  3. Confirm orientation visually against at least two obvious landmarks (lake shore, ridge crest, road bend or valley slope) before trusting the match.[N14, N03, ED]

Failure recognition · Holding the map right-side-up to read labels instead of aligning north to the landscape, causing left-right turn reversals at trail forks.[N08, ED]

Verify · Point from your map position toward two visible landmarks and verify both match real-world direction and distance roughly.[N03, N08, ED]

Stop or change course · If compass needle orientation contradicts major visible terrain features, step away from metal/iron rock and re-verify your location before moving.[N08, T06]

N03 · USGS · What is a Topographic Map (current web edition)
Section: What is a Topographic Map > Contours and Relief · Conditions: Matching three-dimensional terrain shapes to two-dimensional contour lines
Support: Closed contour rings denote peaks or depressions (hachured), V-patterns point uphill in valleys/drainages and wide spacing marks gentle slopes while tight spacing marks cliffs or steep grades.
Limitations: Dense forest canopy or snow drifts can obscure subtle terrain features shown on the map. · Conflicting evidence: None; core topographic terrain-association evidence.

Section: NOAA Magnetic Declination > definition and true-bearing conversion · Conditions: Using magnetic baseplate compasses near metal, electronics or iron-rich rock

N14 · USGS · How To Get Your Map and Compass To Face the Same Direction (current web edition (undated))
Section: How To Get Your Map and Compass To Face the Same Direction (orientation steps) · Conditions: Orienting a paper map with a baseplate compass
Support: Place the baseplate parallel to the map north-south neat line; turn the dial until ring north agrees with map north and correct for declination (subtract if positive, add if negative; an adjustable compass already calibrated needs no correction); turn map and compass together until the red needle overlays the orienting arrow, avoiding metal. The map is then oriented to true north; compare surrounding features with the map.
Limitations: Undated web page. The declination value must come from a current model or map margin. Does not address grid north. · Conflicting evidence: None; consistent with the N08 and T08 declination convention.
