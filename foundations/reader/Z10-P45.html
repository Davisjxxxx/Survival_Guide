Distinguish early frostnip from superficial and deep frostbite

Catch reversible cold-skin warning cues early and recognize true freezing injury before tissue loss deepens.

When it applies · Sub-freezing air, wind chill, metal/fuel contact, or wet winter gloves/boots affecting fingers, toes, ears, nose, or cheeks.

Equipment · Windproof shelter, dry mittens/socks, buddy face-check routine, and warm trunk-contact awareness.

Actions

  1. Watch buddies' cheeks, nose, and ears frequently—early frostnip shows pale/red cold skin with stinging or tingling that remains soft and pliable and responds promptly to shelter and gentle skin-to-skin warmth (such as tucking bare fingers into your own armpit).[T04, E15]
  2. Recognize true frostbite cues: waxy white, grayish, or mottled skin, numbness ("wooden" loss of feeling), and firm or hard-frozen underlying tissue—remembering field inspection cannot reliably determine final tissue depth.[T04, M24]
  3. Stop exposed travel immediately upon finding numb or waxy skin, shield from wind, remove rings/watches/tight boots before swelling begins, and never rub frozen skin with snow or bare hands.[M24, T04]

Failure recognition · Ignoring numb toes inside damp boots for hours or rubbing white waxy cheeks with snow, which mechanically shreds frozen skin cells.[M24, T04]

Verify · Confirm buddy face/hand checks occurred, jewelry/tight items are removed, and wind exposure has stopped.[M24, E15]

Stop or change course · Numb waxy or hard-frozen skin requires immediate trip abort, protection from trauma/refreezing, and clinical frostbite evaluation.[M24, T04]

Evidence and editorial notes
E15 · NWS · Cold Weather Safety (current web edition)
Section: Cold Weather Safety > Wind Chill and Dressing for Cold · Conditions: Cold, windy conditions where exposed skin cools rapidly
Support: Wind removes the thin warm air boundary layer around skin and permeable fabrics, sharply accelerating heat loss and frostbite onset; windproof outer shells and sheltered microclimates mitigate convective loss.
Limitations: Wind chill charts apply to exposed skin and do not change freezing point of water or equipment. · Conflicting evidence: None; clarifies physical mechanism of convective heat loss.
Editorial note: Supports wind-block geometry, shell layering, and wind-chill risk decisions. · Specialist reviewer: Cold-weather shelter and meteorology

Section: Field treatment, avoid refreezing; rapid water-bath rewarming · Conditions: Suspected frozen tissue without safe definitive care
Support: Protect from refreezing and mechanical injury. Any active rewarming requires the guideline’s conditions and a safe no-refreezing plan.
Limitations: Temperature, duration and transfer are clinical decisions; avoid converting the guideline into a universal field recipe. · Conflicting evidence: See A02 claim audit for boundaries.

Section: Field treatment scenarios and Table 1 · Conditions: Suspected frostbite outside immediate medical care
