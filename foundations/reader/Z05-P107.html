Dryad Saddle / Pheasant Back (Cerioporus squamosus) Polypore Profile

Document diagnostic traits of Cerioporus squamosus and contrasting lookalikes under permanent consumption HOLD.

When it applies · Dead or dying Minnesota elms, maples, boxelders; Spring-Autumn (Scaly tan fan cap & large angular pores).

Equipment · Mycology key, cm ruler, black-and-white spore card, paper bag.

Actions

  1. Verify Cerioporus squamosus ((Huds.) Quél.; syn. Polyporus squamosus) via Bell Museum Fungarium and MyCoPortal: Large fan-to-kidney-shaped tan cap patterned with dark brown feathery scales; distinct large angular white-to-cream honeycomb pores running down a stout dark velvety off-center stem; fresh cucumber or watermelon-rind odor; white spore print.[B05, B19, B24]
  2. Exclude lookalikes (Gilled wood mushrooms, tough woody Ganoderma or Fomes brackets); reject on: True blade-like gills; missing dark velvety stem base; woody leathery mature brackets; urban sprayed trees.[B05, B19, B24, B06, B08]
  3. Separate recognition from eating (Verify large angular pores and velvety dark stem base cleanly; consumption strictly WITHHELD); obey non-SNA and clean-site rules under WITHHELD_HOLD.[B05, B24]

Failure recognition · Single-trait guessing or common names confuse Cerioporus squamosus with Gilled wood mushrooms, risking poisoning.[B03, B06, B24]

Verify · Binomial, multi-trait key match, lookalike exclusions, season and habitat, and WITHHELD_HOLD status are logged.[B05, B24]

Stop or change course · Stop if any key trait is missing or ambiguous; call Minnesota Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) or 911 on suspected ingestion.[B09, B24]
