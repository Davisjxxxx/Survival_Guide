Z10-P04 · Wilderness medicine · New manuscript

Apply commercial tourniquet limits and EMS time documentation

Distinguish validated commercial extremity tourniquets from unsafe improvised straps when direct pressure fails.

When it applies · Life-threatening bleeding from a visible arm or leg wound when a suitable commercial tourniquet is available.

Equipment · Commercial windlass tourniquet (e.g., CAT/SOFTT), permanent marker or written log, and watch.

Actions

  1. For life-threatening arm or leg bleeding with a visible wound, follow training and the device directions. Place a commercial tourniquet 2 to 3 inches toward the torso from the wound, clear of any joint, and tighten until bleeding stops.[M02]
  2. Lock the windlass securely, write the exact application time on the tourniquet strap and patient log, and leave the tourniquet uncovered and clearly visible for arriving EMS crews.[M02, M20]
  3. Leave the tourniquet in place for medical personnel. Do not loosen it during evacuation.[M02]

Failure recognition · Relying on shoelaces, thin paracord, or loose belts that pinch skin without occluding arterial flow, or hiding the tourniquet under a blanket without recording application time.[M02]

Verify · Confirm bleeding has ceased completely, application time is written clearly, and urgent EMS evacuation is activated.[M02, T02]

Stop or change course · If bleeding continues after correct placement and maximum tightening, follow your training or dispatcher instructions. The Red Cross describes a second commercial tourniquet above the first when available.[M02]

Evidence and editorial notes
M02 · American Red Cross · How to Apply a Tourniquet (Current web edition)
Section: How to Apply a Tourniquet; Windlass Rod Tourniquet · Conditions: Life-threatening extremity bleeding, visible wound, suitable commercial device
Support: Place the device 2–3 inches toward the torso from the wound, between wound and heart, clear of joints. Tighten until bleeding stops and leave in place for medical personnel.
Limitations: Identify a visible wound and follow the exact device and training. This is not the tactical high-and-tight rule or a pediatric device-fit approval. · Conflicting evidence: See A02 claim audit for boundaries.
Editorial note: Governs extremity hemorrhage escalation and strict EMS handoff documentation. · Specialist reviewer: Trauma EMS and wilderness medicine

M20 · NIH / MedlinePlus · First Aid Overview (current web edition)
Section: Lay First-Aid Scope, Kit Readiness, and Documentation · Conditions: Pre-trip kit preparation and remote patient monitoring
Support: Maintain an inspected, unexpired first-aid kit matched to your actual training, keep written patient observations (time, responsiveness, breathing, symptoms, interventions) for EMS handoff, and practice skills in certified courses.
Limitations: Carrying advanced medical instruments without clinical training adds risk rather than capability. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with ARC and NPS-K.
Editorial note: Anchors kit sizing, SOAP-style lay observation logs, and scope-of-practice cards. · Specialist reviewer: Wilderness EMS and clinical education

T02 · American Red Cross · First Aid Steps (current web edition)
Section: First Aid Steps > Call 9-1-1 · Conditions: Lay emergency response to unresponsiveness, abnormal breathing or severe bleeding
Support: Activate EMS and get needed equipment. Follow dispatcher directions and act within training.
Limitations: Remote locations may delay EMS arrival, requiring sustained stabilization after activation. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with NPS-E.
Editorial note: Mandates early emergency activation and dispatcher adherence. · Specialist reviewer: EMS and wilderness medicine
