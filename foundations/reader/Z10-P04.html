Z10-P04 · Wilderness medicine · New manuscript

Apply commercial tourniquet limits and EMS time documentation

Distinguish validated commercial extremity tourniquets from unsafe improvised straps when direct pressure fails.

When it applies · Life-threatening arm or leg bleeding uncontrolled by firm direct pressure when a commercial windlass tourniquet and training are available.

Equipment · Commercial windlass tourniquet (e.g., CAT/SOFTT), permanent marker or written log, and watch.

Actions

  1. Place a commercial tourniquet high and tight on the injured arm or leg—at least 2 to 3 inches above the wound and NEVER directly over a knee or elbow joint—and tighten the windlass until bright bleeding stops completely.[M02]
  2. Lock the windlass securely, write the exact application time on the tourniquet strap and patient log, and leave the tourniquet uncovered and clearly visible for arriving EMS crews.[M02, M20]
  3. Never loosen or remove a tourniquet placed for life-threatening bleeding in the field; hospital surgical teams manage definitive removal.[M02]

Failure recognition · Relying on shoelaces, thin paracord, or loose belts that pinch skin without occluding arterial flow, or hiding the tourniquet under a blanket without recording application time.[M02]

Verify · Confirm bleeding has ceased completely, application time is written clearly, and urgent EMS evacuation is activated.[M02, T02]

Stop or change course · If bleeding persists after tightening one properly placed commercial tourniquet, apply a second commercial tourniquet directly above the first if available and expedite EMS rescue.[M02, T02]

Section: Wound Packing and Commercial Tourniquet Application · Conditions: Severe extremity bleeding uncontrolled by direct pressure; trained responder with commercial windlass tourniquet
Support: Apply a commercial tourniquet high and tight on an extremity (never over a joint), tighten until bleeding stops, note the application time, and leave it in place for EMS.
Limitations: Improvised tourniquets fail frequently; formal hands-on training is required before operational reliance. · Conflicting evidence: None; strictly distinguishes commercial tourniquets from improvised bands.
Editorial note: Governs extremity hemorrhage escalation and strict EMS handoff documentation. · Specialist reviewer: Trauma EMS and wilderness medicine

Section: Lay First-Aid Scope, Kit Readiness, and Documentation · Conditions: Pre-trip kit preparation and remote patient monitoring

Limitations: Carrying advanced medical instruments without clinical training adds risk rather than capability. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with ARC and NPS-K.
