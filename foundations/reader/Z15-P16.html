Move immediately to lowest interior tornado shelter on Tornado Warnings

Put maximum structural walls and earth between occupants and deadly flying tornado debris within seconds of a Warning.

When it applies · Homes, apartments, workplaces, and schools under an active NWS Tornado Warning.

Equipment · Pre-designated safe spot, bicycle/sports helmets for head protection, sturdy closed-toe shoes, flashlight, whistle, and NOAA/phone alerts.

Actions

  1. On a Tornado Warning, move IMMEDIATELY to a FEMA-rated storm shelter/safe room or underground basement (getting beneath a sturdy workbench or interior staircase away from heavy overhead appliances like pianos/fridges on the floor above).[D03, E17]
  2. If NO basement exists, move to the SMALLEST INTERIOR ROOM ON THE LOWEST FLOOR (interior bathroom, closet, or central hallway) with as many walls between you and the outdoors as possible—staying strictly AWAY from windows, exterior doors, and wide-span garage/gym roofs.[D03, E17]
  3. Keep sturdy closed-toe shoes, a flashlight, a rescue whistle, and helmets/thick blankets staged in your tornado shelter spot so occupants can protect heads/necks immediately and walk safely across broken glass afterward.[D03, T12]

Failure recognition · Stopping to crack open windows ("to equalize pressure"—a dangerous myth!) or staying in an upstairs bedroom watching the storm approach.[D03]

Verify · Confirm lowest interior safe spot chosen ahead of time, shoes, helmets, whistle, and flashlight staged inside, and windows avoided completely.[D03]

Stop or change course · Stay in shelter until the official Tornado Warning expires or radar confirms the storm has safely passed—secondary vortexes and hail can follow.[D03]

D03 · FEMA / Ready.gov · Tornadoes (current web edition)
Section: Tornadoes > Watch vs Warning, Safe Room Selection, and Post-Storm Hazards · Conditions: Developing supercells, Tornado Watches, and Tornado Warnings
Support: Act immediately on a Tornado Warning by moving to a FEMA safe room, storm shelter, basement, or lowest-level small interior room away from windows; protect head/neck from flying debris, never shelter in vehicles/mobile homes or under overpasses, and watch for downed power lines and gas leaks afterward.
Limitations: Outdoor sirens are designed to alert people outdoors and may be inaudible inside insulated homes during storms. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with E17 and NWS-TORNADO.
Editorial note: Core tornado shelter-selection and post-storm hazard evidence in Z15. · Specialist reviewer: Meteorology and emergency management

E17 · NWS · Tornado Safety (current web edition)
Section: Tornado Safety > Staying Safe During a Tornado · Conditions: Tornado warning in camp, vehicle, or building
Support: Move to an interior lower-level room of a sturdy building if reachable; tents, mobile structures, and highway overpasses are unsafe; if caught outdoors with no sturdy shelter, monitor alerts early and follow NWS last-resort guidance.
Limitations: No outdoor improvised shelter is tornado-safe. · Conflicting evidence: None; reinforces early forecast withdrawal before severe storms arrive.
Editorial note: Establishes severe-storm abandonment threshold for camp shelters. · Specialist reviewer: Emergency management and meteorology
Primary source · checked 2026-11-24
T12 · FEMA / Ready.gov · Build a Kit (current web edition)
Section: Build a Kit > Basic Disaster Supplies Kit and Maintenance · Conditions: Overnight and multi-day emergency readiness
Support: Assemble water, food, light, NOAA/battery radio, first aid, whistle, local maps, sanitation, medications, and backup power; inspect expiration dates and batteries regularly.
Limitations: Standard 72-hour kits do not cover extended multi-day isolation without replenishment or specialized cold/medical items. · Conflicting evidence: None; links kit readiness to periodic maintenance checks.
