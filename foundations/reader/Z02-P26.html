Z02-P26 · Shelter and housing · Adapted historical material

Stack foam and inflatable pads for cold ground insulation

Maintain reliable underbody thermal separation on frozen soil or snow and protect against inflatable-pad puncture failure.

When it applies · Cold-weather overnight stops, snow camping and chilly ground emergencies.

Equipment · Closed-cell foam sleeping pad, insulated inflatable pad if carried, patch kit and dry ground barrier.

Actions

  1. Combine a closed-cell foam pad underneath an insulated inflatable pad in cold conditions to increase ground separation and shield the air pad from punctures.[E05, E20, E09]
  2. Inspect inflatable valves and seams before bedtime and avoid over-pressurizing or dragging air pads over pine cones, gravel or sharp frozen twigs.[E20, ED]
  3. Keep underbody insulation centered beneath shoulders, hips and torso—not just the head—using folded dry spare clothing or a dry pack under feet if a short pad is used.[E05, E09, ED]

Failure recognition · Relying on a single uninsulated air mattress on frozen ground, where convective air movement inside the pad drains core warmth all night.[E05, E20]

Verify · Lie briefly in full sleep position to confirm no shoulder, hip or knee presses directly against cold ground or deflated chambers.[E05, ED]

Stop or change course · If an inflatable pad fails in freezing weather and no dry foam or backup ground insulation keeps the sleeper warm, switch to dry natural/clothing buffers and reassess exit or rescue.[E05, E06, ED]
