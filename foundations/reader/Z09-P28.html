Z09-P28 · Tools and construction · New manuscript

Use an axe or hatchet only on a low stable chopping block

Prevent deflection strikes into legs and feet when splitting or limbing small camp wood with a swinging edge.

When it applies · Trained operators using a maintained camp axe or hatchet on an authorized site.

Equipment · Inspected sharp axe/hatchet with tight head, broad flat chopping block, clear work circle and sturdy boots.

Actions

  1. Place the workpiece firmly against a broad, solid low chopping block so any miss or pass-through buries cleanly into wood—never chop against bare soil, rock or elevated knee-height logs.[K03, K08, ED]
  2. Keep your stance spread wide or kneel safely with the chopping block between the cutting arc and your legs so an over-swing strikes the block or ground safely short of shins and feet.[K03, K08, ED]
  3. Use the contact-split method for short kindling blocks: press the hatchet bit squarely into the end grain first, lift wood and hatchet together as one unit and bring both down together onto the chopping block—keeping your free hand completely away from the blade.[K02, K03, ED]

Failure recognition · Holding a short stove-length billet upright with fingers two inches from the top while swinging a hatchet down at it.[K02, K03]

Verify · Confirm tight head wedge, clear work circle, broad low chopping block and free hand strictly away from the impact zone.[K03, K08, ED]

Stop or change course · Never swing an axe or hatchet when tired, chilled, rushed or in dim twilight—sheath it and use a folding saw or hand-broken dead twigs instead.[K03, K08, E06]
