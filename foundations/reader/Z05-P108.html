Chaga (Inonotus obliquus) Birch Canker Conservation & Oxalate Limits

Document diagnostic traits of Inonotus obliquus and contrasting lookalikes under permanent consumption HOLD.

When it applies · Living paper and yellow birch trees in northern Minnesota; Year-round (Charred black exterior & rusty-orange core).

Equipment · Mycology key, cm ruler, black-and-white spore card, paper bag.

Actions

  1. Verify Inonotus obliquus ((Fr.) Pilát; syn. Sterile birch conk) via Bell Museum Fungarium and MyCoPortal: Hard irregular black charcoal-like sterile fungal canker protruding from living birch trunks with a dense corky golden-rust interior; slow-growing parasite taking years to form.[B05, B21, B22, B23]
  2. Exclude lookalikes (Woody tree burls (solid swirling wood grain lacking rusty cork interior), Fomes fomentarius (smooth hoof-shaped tinder conk)); reject on: Chaga contains extremely high soluble oxalates linked to documented oxalate nephropathy or kidney failure; hacking live birch trees in State or National Parks or SNAs is strictly illegal.[B05, B21, B22, B23, B06, B08]
  3. Separate recognition from eating (Documented high-oxalate kidney injury risk and live-tree park-harvest bans; consumption strictly WITHHELD); obey non-SNA and clean-site rules under WITHHELD_HOLD.[B05, B24]

Failure recognition · Single-trait guessing or common names confuse Inonotus obliquus with Woody tree burls, risking poisoning.[B03, B06, B24]

Verify · Binomial, multi-trait key match, lookalike exclusions, season and habitat, and WITHHELD_HOLD status are logged.[B05, B24]

Stop or change course · Stop if any key trait is missing or ambiguous; call Minnesota Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) or 911 on suspected ingestion.[B09, B24]
