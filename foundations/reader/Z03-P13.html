Z03-P13 · Fire and ignition · New manuscript

Balance heat, fuel and oxygen in a controlled small fire

Manage ignition energy, dry fuel sizing and open airflow so an authorized small fire burns cleanly without escalating.

When it applies · Lawful outdoor campfires in approved rings during permitted weather conditions.

Equipment · Pre-staged dry tinder, small kindling, modest deadwood fuel, water and stirring tool.

Actions

  1. Recognize that sustained combustion requires all three elements together—sufficient heat, dry finely divided fuel and unobstructed oxygen flow—removing any one stops the flame.[C03, C04, ED]
  2. Keep initial flame small and open to air rather than piling thick logs tightly onto tiny sparks, which suffocates oxygen and chills the ignition point.[C03, C17]
  3. Maintain a modest manageable fire size suited to cooking or brief warmth; oversized fires multiply ember loft, radiant hazard and water needed for full extinguishment.[C02, C04, C17]

Failure recognition · Smothering a weak tinder flame with damp thick sticks or stacking high logs that throw sparks outside the fire ring.[C02, C03, C17]

Verify · Confirm steady small flame with clean upward draft, zero overhanging branches and extinguishing water within arm's reach.[C02, C04, ED]

Stop or change course · If wind gusts scatter sparks, fuel burns too large to manage or smoke blows across shelters, drown and stir the fire cold immediately.[C02, C04, C18]

C02 · USDA Forest Service / Smokey Bear · Smokey Bear’s Campfire Safety Guide (agency PDF)
Section: Build your campfire; choose a location · Conditions: Where campfires are currently permitted
Support: Use an established fire ring where required or available. Protect the area from nearby combustible materials, avoid windy conditions and keep water ready.
Limitations: Site clearances vary by fuel load, slope, and wind; no universal safe radius fits all terrain. · Conflicting evidence: None; aligns with USFS-FIRE.
Editorial note: Grounds site-selection and ring-preparation procedures. · Specialist reviewer: Fire prevention and land management

C04 · USDA Forest Service / Smokey Bear · Smokey Bear’s Campfire Safety Guide (agency PDF)
Section: Maintain and extinguish a campfire · Conditions: Occupied campfire, water and tools available
Support: Stay with the fire. Drown, stir and add water again until cool. Leaving visible embers or merely covering them is unsafe.
Limitations: Deep duff or peat soils can hold smoldering embers underground even after surface ash cools. · Conflicting evidence: None; prohibits dirt-only burial as primary extinguishment.
Editorial note: Enforces drown-stir-feel-cold cycle before site departure. · Specialist reviewer: Fire prevention and land management
Primary source · checked 2026-11-24
C17 · NPS · Leave No Trace Seven Principles (current web edition)
Section: Principle 5 > Minimize Campfire Impacts · Conditions: Permitted backcountry sites where wood fires are allowed
Support: Where fires are permitted, keep fires small, use only dead and downed wood gathered lawfully, burn wood completely to ash, and use a camp stove where wood is scarce or impacts are high.
Limitations: Some parks prohibit all wood gathering or open fires regardless of deadfall availability. · Conflicting evidence: None; balances lawful fuel collection with habitat protection.
Editorial note: Guides lawful fuel gathering and small-fire thermal management. · Specialist reviewer: Land management and fire prevention
Primary source · checked 2026-11-24
C18 · NWS · Wildfire Weather Safety (current web edition)
Section: Wildfire Weather Safety > Red Flag and Wind Conditions · Conditions: Low humidity, dry fuels, and gusty winds
Support: Red Flag Warnings and high wind advisories signal extreme fire-spread potential where embers travel downwind and small sparks rapidly escape containment.
Limitations: Local gusts can exceed forecast averages along ridges and open clearings. · Conflicting evidence: None; reinforces wind-abort threshold before ignition.
Editorial note: Establishes Red Flag / high-wind conditions as automatic no-fire triggers. · Specialist reviewer: Wildland fire safety and meteorology
