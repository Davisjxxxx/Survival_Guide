Z04-P33 · Water · Adapted historical material

Pre-filter coarse particles with cloth or coffee filter before treatment

Remove suspended grit, leaves and organic clumps cleanly to protect porous filters and improve disinfection reliability.

When it applies · Turbid or debris-laden raw water from a chemically acceptable source prior to boiling, filtration or chemical disinfection.

Equipment · Clean woven cloth, bandana, paper coffee filter or mesh pre-filter, raw catch vessel and validated microbial treatment.

Actions

  1. Secure a clean cloth, layered paper towel or coffee filter over the mouth of a dedicated raw container and pour settled raw water through slowly.[W08, W24]
  2. Replace or rinse clogged pre-filter media away from the clean side rather than squeezing muddy sludge forcefully through the weave.[W08, W24, ED]
  3. Treat pre-filtered water immediately as still microbiologically unsafe—cloth and paper never stop bacteria, viruses or protozoan cysts.[W08, W09, W19, W20]

Failure recognition · Squeezing a muddy bandana into a drinking bottle or treating coffee-filtered clear-looking water as potable.[W08, W09]

Verify · Coarse debris is retained on the raw cloth while the strained water enters a validated microbial treatment step.[W08, ED]

Stop or change course · Reject cloth pre-filtration as a standalone treatment; if no validated microbial barrier is available after straining, do not treat the water as potable.[W08, W09]

W08 · CDC · How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency (2024-09-19)
Section: Boil > Steps for boiling water > cloudy water · Conditions: Cloudy water with no identified chemical hazard
Support: Settling or cloth clarification only prepares water for treatment. Do not count it as disinfection.
Limitations: Removes coarse suspended solids only; dissolved toxins and microbes remain. · Conflicting evidence: Contradicts folklore claims that sand/cloth straining yields potable water; CDC governs.
Editorial note: Always depicted upstream of microbial treatment with an explicit barrier line. · Specialist reviewer: Drinking-water safety

Section: Drinking water and preventing infection while hiking or camping · Conditions: Untreated surface water in wilderness and rural watersheds
Support: Use boiling or a parasite-rated filter. Chemical treatment effectiveness depends on product, target organism, temperature and contact time.
Limitations: Pore size alone does not protect if cartridge seals leak or freeze-crack. · Conflicting evidence: None; underscores why chemical-only treatment has parasite limits.

W20 · CDC · Choosing Home Water Filters and Other Water Treatment Systems (current web edition)
Section: Removing parasites; Check the filter label · Conditions: Surface water impacted by wildlife, livestock, or runoff
Support: For parasite removal look for absolute pore size one micron or smaller or a specific certified cyst reduction/removal claim. NSF/ANSI 53 or 58 without the cyst claim does not establish that capability.
Limitations: Does not establish removal of viruses, dissolved chemicals or all other hazards. · Conflicting evidence: Clarifies gap left by single-barrier chlorine disinfection.
