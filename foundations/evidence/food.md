# Food safety and preservation · evidence pack

Checked 2026-11-24. U.S. household and emergency conditions. Use F01–F07 in the [claim register](claims.tsv) and agency editions in the [source register](sources.tsv).

| Decision | Source-supported boundary | Editorial implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare food | Clean hands and surfaces, keep raw foods separate, check food-specific internal temperature and refrigerate promptly. | Keep the individual temperature table with the governing source. Avoid visual cook checks. |
| Control time outside refrigeration | Use the two-hour limit, or one hour above 90°F, under the official general guidance. | Record cooling and warm exposure separately. Verify conditions rather than estimating from smell or taste. |
| Manage outages | A closed fridge keeps food cold for about four hours. Approximate freezer durations are 48 hours full and 24 hours half-full. Per-item time/temperature rules and ice crystals matter. | Outage card records the measured value, elapsed time and category before a keep/refreeze/discard decision. |
| Preserve shelf-stable food | Low-acid foods require an applicable tested pressure-canning process. Recipe, acidity, container and elevation affect the validated process. | The reconstruction teaches recipe selection with no replacement schedule. |

A food-safety reviewer should check item-specific outage criteria, high-risk populations and the match between tested recipe and equipment. Keep recipe and chart versions available offline before any instructional expansion.
