# Environmental exposure and shelter · evidence pack

Checked 2026-11-24. Outdoor exposure examples draw on Minnesota and U.S. federal sources. E01–E14 record conditions and locators in the [claim register](claims.tsv), with dated entries in the [source register](sources.tsv).

| Scenario | Source-supported boundary | Editorial implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Cold, wet and windy return | Wind and cold-ground contact matter. Wet clothing and overexertion can increase heat loss. Carry dry insulation and an appropriate shelter. | Stage a dry reserve, confirm ground isolation and keep an identifiable exit. Draw the contact layers rather than rating warmth. |
| Site and drainage | Flash floods can be driven by upstream rain. Avoid channel sites. Trees and branches can fall. | Test how the site changes when runoff, wind or snow increases. Keep an exit to stable higher ground as appropriate. |
| Lightning | At thunder, seek a substantial building or hard-topped enclosed vehicle. Wait 30 minutes after last thunder. | Plan ahead for the actual refuge. Screens, tents and natural windbreaks are weather protection only. |
| Heat | Cool breaks and reduced exertion can lower heat strain. Emergency signs require escalation. | Identify a practical refuge and check the slowest group member. Use the separate triage pack for medical criteria. |
| Ice and vehicles | Surface ice varies. A stranded vehicle can provide protection in winter if its location is safe and no obvious safe refuge is nearby. | Stay on verified land routes. Keep the winter vehicle card separate from any engine-use directions. |

Independent review should address shelter stability, avalanche terrain, enclosed shelter ventilation, equipment ratings, and contraindications. Those domains are outside the illustrated structural claims in this batch.
