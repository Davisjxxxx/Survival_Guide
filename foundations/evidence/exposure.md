# Environmental exposure and shelter · evidence pack

Checked 2026-11-24. Upper Midwest and U.S. federal outdoor exposure, weather, and shelter-safety sources. Use E01–E27 in the [claim register](claims.tsv) and dated entries in the [source register](sources.tsv).

| Scenario | Source-supported boundary | Editorial implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Site, terrain, and drainage | Choose legal durable sites (E03, E21, E22) above stream channels/floodplains (E04), clear of hazard trees (E13), wildlife food-storage zones (E27), and avalanche slopes/runout paths (E23). | Use slope/drainage selection instead of destructive trenching; exclude unvalidated fall-zone distances and avalanche terrain. |
| Wind, precipitation, and temperature | Wind accelerates convective cooling (E15) and stresses shelters/trees (E16); wet layers and sweat accelerate hypothermia (E05, E06, E10); unventilated tents trap solar heat (E19, E26). | Separate conductive, convective, radiative, and evaporative heat pathways; document manufactured vs improvised shelter limits without unvalidated load specs. |
| Severe storms and lightning | At thunder move to a substantial building or hard-topped enclosed vehicle and wait 30 minutes after last thunder (E07); vacate lightweight shelters before outflow gusts, hail, or tornado warnings (E17, E18, E24). | Define explicit abandonment thresholds and hazard-matched shelter classes. |
| Winter, snow, and vehicles | Keep exits and vents clear of drifting snow (E25); stay with a safe stranded vehicle if no obvious refuge is nearby (E14); never cross uncertain ice (E12). | Treat snow trenches/caves as trained-only hazard boundaries with zero unvalidated roof-load claims; enforce CO-free enclosure rules. |

Cold-weather shelter, structural-safety, avalanche, and land-management specialists must review shelter mechanics, material tolerances, and abandonment criteria prior to field authorization.
