# Water sourcing, treatment and contamination · evidence pack

Checked 2026-11-24. United States emergency and backcountry contexts. Use the [claim register](claims.tsv) W01–W11 for original agency locators and limits, and the [source register](sources.tsv) for dated references.

| Decision | Source-supported boundary | Editorial implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Select a supply | Rain, clear surface water and melted snow can carry contaminants. An advisory or suspected toxic contamination can require another source. | Move source screening ahead of vessel and treatment decisions. Keep the collection task separate from the decision to drink. |
| Remove visible sediment | Settling or cloth filtration prepares water for treatment. | Use a separate raw vessel. The diagram keeps sediment removal and microbial treatment apart. |
| Boil | The CDC emergency table gives one minute at a rolling boil and three minutes above 6,500 feet. It excludes toxic chemicals, fuel and radioactive contamination. | Use a timer and correct the older plate's incomplete altitude context. Test a dry run with no drinking assignment. |
| Filter and disinfect | The target microbes and product performance determine capability. Common portable filters leave gaps, and chemical treatment performs poorly against some parasites. Iodine has additional user restrictions. | Require exact product instructions, target coverage and demonstrated batch tracking. Generic tablet dosing and unspecified impact of container materials stay out of these cards. |
| Use UV | Cloudiness limits exposure. Device capacity and cycle depend on manufacturer instructions. | Require clear water, supported batch size and a confirmed cycle. |
| Store and check advisory | Treat storage and dispensing as potential recontamination points. Public-water notices impose different limits. | Label raw and potable supplies and record the issuing authority and current notice. |

Independent review should check case boundaries, including cold water and shortened contact time, contaminant-specific equipment claims, sanitation instructions, and the handoff to alternate supplies.
