# Emergency recognition, triage and evacuation · evidence pack

Checked 2026-11-24. Layperson first response, emergency planning, accessibility, and evacuation guidance in the United States. Use T01–T16 in the [claim register](claims.tsv) and dated entries in the [source register](sources.tsv).

| Decision | Source-supported boundary | Editorial implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Scene safety and EMS activation | Verify scene safety before approaching; call 911 for unresponsiveness, abnormal breathing, or severe bleeding and follow dispatcher instructions (T01, T02). | Assign caller and scene roles separately; keep layperson escalation distinct from clinical Z10 scope. |
| Exposure red flags | Confusion, slurred speech, fumbling, or drowsiness signal hypothermia (T03) or heat stroke (T05, T09); frostbite active rewarming depends on preventing refreezing (T04). | Pair behavioral recognition directly with stop conditions and emergency activation. |
| Communication and alerts | Layer out-of-area contacts, paper plans, SMS/voice channels, WEA alerts, and battery/crank NOAA Weather Radio (T11, T14, T15); distinguish Watches from Warnings (T16). | Account for backcountry signal shadows and require confirmation before assuming rescue dispatch. |
| Accessible planning and evacuation | Build a personal support network, accessible transport, voluntary local registry enrollment, medication/power backups, and service-animal supplies (T10); prepare multi-day kits (T12) and evacuate early on official orders (T07, T13). | Embed access and functional needs directly in Z01 fundamentals rather than treating them as an afterthought. |

EMS, search-and-rescue, accessibility, and emergency-management specialists must review decision thresholds, communication protocols, and clinical boundaries prior to field authorization.
