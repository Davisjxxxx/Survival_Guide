# Emergency recognition, triage and evacuation · evidence pack

Checked 2026-11-24. Layperson first response and escalation in the U.S. Use T01–T09 in the [claim register](claims.tsv) and agency and specialist references in the [source register](sources.tsv).

| Decision | Source-supported boundary | Editorial implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Check and activate help | Scene safety and responsiveness, breathing and life-threatening bleeding inform immediate EMS escalation. | Give the caller and scene roles separately. The lay card prioritizes recognition and dispatcher directions. |
| Cold | Altered behavior, fumbling and drowsiness in cold exposure can indicate serious illness. Frostbite treatment decisions depend on refreezing risk and evacuation. | Pair recognition and escalation with protection against further cooling. Qualified review must precede detailed clinical treatment. |
| Heat | Confusion, slurred speech and reduced alertness after heat exposure can indicate heat stroke. Prompt EMS and cooling are required. | Put symptoms and urgent actions next to each other. Choose a feasible cool refuge before exertion. |
| Search and rescue | Last known position, access details and state of the party help responders. Known safe route and immediate hazards influence stay/move decisions. | Use precise place names and identify uncertainty. Do not assume a message was received. |
| Evacuation | Follow official evacuation direction and avoid flooded routes. | Use prior contact and needs records and update plans as roads and hazards change. |

Navigation interpretation and evacuation depend on actual conditions and geographic references. Clinical methods, patient categories and regional EMS protocols require specialist dispositions before expansion.
