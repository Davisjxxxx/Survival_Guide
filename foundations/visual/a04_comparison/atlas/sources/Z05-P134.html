Report Existing Evidence After Suspected Plant or Mushroom Exposure

Share what you already know with Poison Control or responders without delaying care or risking a new exposure.

When it applies · Immediately following any suspected toxic plant, berry, root, or wild mushroom ingestion.

Equipment · Phone and existing exposure information, if safely available.

Actions

  1. Call Poison Control promptly. Tell them what is already safely available, such as a photo or food remnant, and follow the specialist’s instructions.[B09, M16]
  2. Use photographs you can take safely without touching suspect material, revisiting a risky location or delaying care.[B02, M16]
  3. Leave collection and transport decisions to Poison Control or responders. Do not expose another person to obtain a specimen.[B09, M16]

Failure recognition · Searching for a new specimen can delay care or expose another person.[B09, M16]

Verify · Confirm that Poison Control or responders have the information already available. Follow their instructions.[B09, M16]

Stop or change course · Call 911 for collapse, seizure, breathing trouble or inability to wake. Do not gather samples at the expense of care or safety.[B09, M15]

Section: When to call 911; get help from poison experts · Conditions: Suspected poisoning in the United States
Support: Call 911 for collapse, seizure, trouble breathing or inability to wake. Otherwise promptly call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222.
Limitations: Use route- and substance-specific advice. Do not wait for symptoms or collect unknown material if doing so risks exposure or delays care. · Conflicting evidence: Conditions are recorded in the A03 audit.
